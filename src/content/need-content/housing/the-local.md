You are not choosing an apartment. You are choosing a ten-minute walking radius, and then taking whatever apartment happens to be inside it. Get that order wrong and you will spend two years in a very nice unit in a place that never becomes yours.

The thing that makes this workable in Boston is that the city is genuinely a stack of small towns that happen to touch. Jamaica Plain, Union Square, Eastie, Roslindale, Inman, and Charlestown all still behave like towns — a main street, a handful of places that everybody in a half-mile uses, and enough foot traffic that the same faces recur. That recurrence is the whole product. You cannot buy it and you cannot rush it, but you can move somewhere it is possible.

### How to actually pick

Go on a Tuesday evening, not a Saturday. Saturday tells you what a neighborhood looks like when it is performing. Tuesday tells you whether anyone is out. Walk from the apartment to the nearest grocery store, the nearest bar, and the nearest T stop, in that order, and time all three. If the grocery walk is over twelve minutes you will start driving, and once you start driving you stop being on the street, and once you are off the street none of this works.

Then check what happens at 9pm. A block that empties at eight is a block where your evening life will happen somewhere else, which means it will happen by appointment, which is the opposite of what you came for.

### The calendar

Boston's rental market is bent almost entirely around September 1. Student leases set the clock, listings for September start appearing in the spring, and the first of the month is a citywide furniture apocalypse — Allston Christmas, though it happens everywhere. That cycle is bad for you in one way and good in another: September has the most inventory and the most competition, while November through February has thin inventory and almost no competition. If you can arrive off-cycle, you will get a better apartment for less money and a landlord who is relieved to see you.

### Money

Massachusetts limits what a landlord can collect up front to first month, last month, a security deposit of no more than one month, and the cost of a new lock and key. Even at the legal maximum, that is three or four months of rent before you have slept there once. Broker fees have been the other traditional line item, and the rules on who pays them changed recently — check the current state of that before you assume either way, because it is a four-figure difference.
