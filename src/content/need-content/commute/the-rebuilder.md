For your mode the commute question is really a different question: what makes it easiest to say yes to something at short notice?

That reframing changes the answer. Range is not your constraint — you are not driving to New Hampshire, you are trying to get across town on a Wednesday when a part of you wants to stay home. So optimise for the trips that are most likely to be abandoned.

### Be on a line

Living on a subway line, ideally within a short walk of a stop, does something specific: it removes the decision. There is no parking to think about, no drive home to dread, no reason to leave early. You check the time, you go. Every friction you remove between the impulse and the door is a plan that survives.

The corollary is that a commute involving two transfers is worse for you than for anyone else, because it will also be your evening trip, and the version of you at 7pm on a Wednesday is much more easily talked out of things than the version at 8am.

### Fix the two-mile problem

The trips that get cancelled are almost never the long ones. They are the awkward middle distances — too far to walk, badly served by rail, annoying enough by bus that you talk yourself out of it. Bluebikes, the regional bikeshare, is the specific fix, and it is cheap. A membership costs less than a couple of nights out and directly converts into showing up.

### Know your last train

Boston's service ends earlier than newcomers expect. This is worth learning immediately, not because you should structure your life around it, but because not knowing produces a particular kind of evening where you leave at nine out of vague anxiety. Learn the actual last departure on your line, budget for one ride share a week, and stop leaving early.

### About the car

If you already have one, keeping it is fine. Just do not let it become the default, because a car in a dense neighborhood quietly discourages spontaneity — you start weighing whether you will lose the parking space, and that weighing is exactly the moment a plan dies.

If you do not have one, do not buy one in year one. Between insurance, a permit that only lets you compete for a space, street cleaning, and winter parking bans, it is a lot of expense and attention for something that does not serve what you are here to do.
