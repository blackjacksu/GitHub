You have a car, and for your mode that is correct. It buys you the thing you actually moved here for: a two-and-a-half-hour radius that contains the White Mountains, the Berkshires, most of the Maine coast, the Cape, and a hundred smaller places nobody has heard of. Nothing else on this list gets you that.

What the car costs you is a set of small, recurring, exclusively local obligations that nobody mentions until you have already been ticketed.

### Parking is a system, and it has rules

**Resident permits** are issued per neighborhood, not per city, and they let you park in permit spaces in your neighborhood. They do not reserve you a space. In the dense areas the ratio of permits to spaces is not close to one.

**Street cleaning** runs roughly spring through fall on a posted schedule that varies by street, often a specific weekday of a specific week of the month. Miss it and you get a ticket, sometimes a tow. Put it in your calendar. This is the single most common way newcomers lose money here.

**Snow emergencies** trigger a parking ban on major streets. The city announces them, and you move the car to a garage or a legal side street before the storm, not during it. Space savers — the folding chair placed in a shovelled-out spot — are a genuine local institution with genuine local rules that vary by neighborhood and by how recently it snowed. Do not test them in your first winter.

### Driving here

The road network predates the automobile in a way you can feel. Expect rotaries, expect intersections with no obvious protocol, expect to be merged into aggressively and to have to merge aggressively back. It sorts itself out once you accept that the local style is decisive rather than polite. Signal, commit, and do not hesitate in a rotary.

Tolls on the Massachusetts Turnpike and the harbor tunnels are all-electronic. Get the transponder; out-of-state plates get billed by mail at worse rates.

Also: check your registration and insurance situation early. Massachusetts requires registration and inspection after you establish residency, insurance rates in the Boston area are high, and there is an annual local excise tax on the vehicle that surprises people every single year.

### The one thing to also do

Get a monthly transit pass or at least a Charlie Card anyway. Not for commuting — for the nights you are going out. The alternative is a whole evening spent thinking about where the car is, which is how people in this mode end up staying home in the city and only feeling alive outside it.
