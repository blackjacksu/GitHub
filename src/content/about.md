I moved to Boston and it took four years before it felt like mine.

Not four years to find an apartment, or learn the T, or figure out where to eat. Those took weeks, and every guide on the internet will help you with them. Four years before there was a place I walked into where someone knew me, a group of people I didn't have to explain myself to, and a week that had a shape.

That gap — between having the information and having a life — is what this site is about.

### Why the checklists don't work

Every relocation guide is organised as a list of independent items. Best neighborhoods. Top restaurants. How to get a Charlie Card. It's all accurate and it all misses, because the items aren't independent.

If you don't have a car, that decides your neighborhood, which decides your Saturday, which decides who you meet. If you're chasing the White Mountains every weekend, a walkable main street is worth less to you than a driveway, and a bar you'd become a regular at is worth almost nothing because you're never there on a Friday. The choices are entangled. A list pretends they aren't.

So the unit here is a **mode of living** — a named way you want your life to go — and everything else is derived from it.

### The actual goal

The goal is not to transfer information faster. It's to manufacture the conditions where *home* happens sooner than it did for me.

As far as I can tell, that came down to three things, and none of them are facts:

- **A place, through repetition.** Somewhere I went so often it stopped being a decision.
- **A small group.** Not a network. Four or five people I didn't have to perform for.
- **A rhythm.** Something recurring that gave the week a shape, so the week wasn't just work with gaps in it.

All three are behavioral. You can't read your way into any of them. Which is why the most important page on this site isn't a guide — it's [Rhythms](/rhythm), a list of recurring things with a nudge to go back a second time. Visit two is where nearly everyone quits, and it's the visit that does the work.

### What this is right now

An early draft with the structure in place and the content only partly written. Housing, commute and friends are written out for all four modes. The rest is summarised. The rhythms are seeded categories rather than verified listings.

The modes themselves are placeholders until they're rebuilt around what actually tipped it — which is the one piece of this that can't be researched, only remembered.

If you just got here: the four years are not inevitable. Most of mine were spent doing visit one over and over.
