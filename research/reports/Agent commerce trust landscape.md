# Nobody Yet Underwrites Agent-to-Agent Commerce

As of October 2026 the "neutral credit bureau for agents" seat that AgentBureau targets is empty but ringed: every payment rail (x402, AP2, Visa TAP, Mastercard Agent Pay, ACP, UCP) has shipped an *authentication* or *authorization* primitive, none publishes a cross-rail *counterparty* score, and the one open reputation substrate (ERC-8004) self-describes its Sybil weakness and is, on the only empirical measurement, majority-Sybil on every chain. The building blocks AgentBureau plans to compose are real and mostly live on Base — ERC-8004 Identity and Reputation registries at `0x8004A169…`/`0x8004BAa1…`, EAS as an OP-Stack predeploy at `0x4200…0021`, x402 v2 under a Linux Foundation foundation, audited spend-permission and delegation contracts — but three of the spec's load-bearing claims need correction (the Validation Registry has no official mainnet address, "85–97% empty shells" is an inversion the paper never states, and ERC-8183 has no audited reference deployment anywhere), two are uncorroborated (the x402 volume trio and UCP's "hundreds of thousands" of merchants), and one (Base's L2 fee share) was not researched at all. The competitive gap is real: no entity found combines operator KYA, a trust score, escrow evaluation and dispute resolution, and ERC-8004's own best-practices document anticipates third parties filling exactly that slot. The scoring literature, however, says the draft AgentBureau formula is built from the weakest ingredients — a symmetric weighted sum dominated by volume and tenure, with stake as a small fixed weight — and should be rebuilt around settled-escrow outcomes, operator-level attribution, counterparty-weighted (asymmetric) volume, and stake that scales with the order limit a band unlocks.

## Read this first: most figures rest on search snippets, not primary pages

## ERC-8004 identities are live on 25 chains, mostly empty, and freely transferable

ERC-8004 "Trustless Agents" remains a **Draft** ERC (created 2025-08-13; authors from MetaMask, the Ethereum Foundation, Google and Coinbase) whose Identity Registry is an upgradeable ERC-721 with URIStorage, so `agentId` is a tokenId and the registration file it points to is a JSON of type `…eip-8004#registration-v1` carrying `services` (A2A, MCP, OASF, ENS, DID, email), `x402Support`, `active` and an optional `supportedTrust` array ([erc-8004.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8004.md); [ERC8004SPEC.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/main/ERC8004SPEC.md)). The official contracts README publishes a single mainnet pair — Identity `0x8004A169FB4a3325136EB29fA0ceB6D2e539a432`, Reputation `0x8004BAa17C55a88189AE136b182e5fdA19dE9b63` — on **25 mainnets including Ethereum, Base and BSC**, and a testnet pair (`0x8004A818…`/`0x8004B663…`) on 25 testnets including Base Sepolia ([erc-8004-contracts](https://github.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts)). The same README lists **no Validation Registry address on any chain**, no version tag, no audit and no proxy-admin information, and states verbatim that the Validation Registry "is still under active update and discussion with the TEE community" and "will be revised and expanded in a follow-up spec update later this year" ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/main/README.md)). A third-party on-chain audit claims a mainnet Validation Registry proxy at `0x8004Cc8439f36fd5F9F049D9fF86523Df6dAAB58` deployed 2026-01-29 on 25 chains with **zero `ValidationRequest` or `ValidationResponse` events** ever on Ethereum ([LLM4Agents](https://llm4agents.com/blog/erc-8004-registries-onchain-audit), snippet); Sperax's deployment table lists a Validation Registry only on BSC testnet and Sepolia ([Sperax docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sperax/erc8004-agents/main/docs/contracts.md)); and a ChaosChain issue reporting that "the Sepolia VR address has no code on Base" was closed "not planned" with no maintainer reply ([ChaosChain#15](https://github.com/ChaosChain/trustless-agents-erc-ri/issues/15)). The same issue reports an ABI mismatch on Base (`agentExists()` reverts while `ownerOf` resolves), a reminder that upgradeable singletons can change semantics without an ERC version bump.

The Reputation Registry's write path is permissive by design. `giveFeedback(agentId, value, valueDecimals, tag1, tag2, endpoint, feedbackURI, feedbackHash)` may be called by any address except the agent's owner or an approved operator; the current spec text has **no `feedbackAuth` parameter** (older drafts did, and several secondary explainers still describe pre-authorization), feedback is revocable by its submitter, the agent may `appendResponse`, and `getSummary` deliberately requires a caller-supplied `clientAddresses` list "to mitigate Sybil and spam attacks" — pushing filtering onto consumers ([erc-8004.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8004.md)). The official best-practices document says the ERC "provides only the feedback data layer" and expects "watchtowers or infrastructure providers" plus "insurers, and auditor networks" to supply reliability signals ([best-practices/Reputation.md](https://github.com/erc-8004/best-practices/blob/main/Reputation.md)) — the bureau role, named by the standard's own authors. A September 2026 contracts issue proposes counting feedback only when backed by verifiable x402 settlement or escrow-release proof, with an `x402-grounded-feedback-v0` schema, and has drawn no maintainer reply ([erc-8004-contracts#99](https://github.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/issues/99)).

Adoption is wide and shallow. The empirical study the spec cites, arXiv 2606.26028 "Can Trustless Agents Be Trusted?", crawled Ethereum, BSC and Base through 2026-05-13 (173,441 agents) and found **only 3%, 4% and 15% of registrations respectively have a valid registration file with a live service endpoint**, that **73.5%, 59.2% and 90.6% of reviewers exhibit coordinated Sybil behavior**, and that after removing flagged feedback **15.8%, 77.9% and 86.8% of rated agents have no valid feedback left** ([arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028), snippet; Sybil figures quoted verbatim in [contracts#99](https://github.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/issues/99)). A separate measure reports 53%/9%/37% of agents on Ethereum/BSC/Base never set a URI at all ([datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/erc-8004-explained), snippet). Independent censuses converge: of 10,000 Ethereum agents only 67 expose service records and 19 combine all tracked signals ([arXiv 2606.12128](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12128v1), snippet); across 26 mainnets BSC holds ~62% of identities but ~2% of feedback while **Base holds ~10% of identities and ~79% of feedback** ([arXiv 2606.25876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25876), snippet); and a 12-chain liveness probe of 392,258 identities found 21.3% declaring routable endpoints and 47.6% of probed identities completing an A2A/MCP handshake ([yaojin0609/erc-8004-liveness](https://github.com/yaojin0609/erc-8004-liveness)). Base is thus simultaneously the chain with the most real agents (15% valid), the worst reviewer Sybil rate (90.6%) and most of the feedback volume — meaning raw Base feedback is the least trustworthy input a Base bureau could ingest. Registration counts span 173k–519k depending on snapshot and chain set; 8004scan reported 519,333 agents across 30 networks on 2026-09-18, with Base at 67,889 ([datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/erc-8004-explained), snippet).

Two structural properties matter for a bureau keyed on agentId. First, because the same vanity address exists on 25 chains with independent tokenId sequences, the globally unique key is the tuple `(agentRegistry = eip155:<chainId>:<address>, agentId)`, never `agentId` alone. Second, the token is freely transferable: on transfer the spec clears only the `agentWallet` metadata while every Reputation and Validation record stays attached to the agentId, so a sold token carries its history to the buyer ([ERC8004SPEC.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/main/ERC8004SPEC.md)). No soulbound variant, transfer restriction or reputation reset exists in the spec; community analyses call this "reputation laundering" and the inverse (abandoning a bad identity, since minting is free) "whitewashing" ([Revettr](https://revettr.com/blog/the-reputation-laundering-problem), snippet; [Concordium](https://www.concordium.com/article/erc-8004-explained-what-it-solves-and-the-gap-it-leaves), snippet). ENSIP-25 adds a name-level attestation: a Draft ENSIP (created 2025-10-02) defines the text record `agent-registration[<ERC-7930 registry address>][<agentId>]`, verified only in the registry-to-ENS direction and, like the registry, not re-attested on NFT transfer ([ensdomains/ensips 25.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ensdomains/ensips/master/ensips/25.md)). A competing design, ERC-8122 "Minimal Agent Registry", lets anyone deploy a registry instead of one singleton per chain and is also transferable ([erc-8122.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8122.md)); ERC-8294 proposes an additive "Validation Network" interface for operator-diverse validator sets ([ethereum/ERCs#1808](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1808)).

Off-chain agent identity is fragmenting along what each scheme binds, and almost none has a slot for an ERC-8004 reference. did:pkh wraps a CAIP-10 account with no rotation or deactivation ([w3c-ccg/did-pkh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/w3c-ccg/did-pkh/main/did-pkh-method-draft.md)); Web Bot Auth binds an Ed25519 key published at `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory` and is now an IETF working-group document (draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00, September 2026) with Cloudflare's own implementations, "not audited" ([cloudflare/web-bot-auth](https://github.com/cloudflare/web-bot-auth); [datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00), snippet); A2A 1.0 serves JWS-signed Agent Cards at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` and mentions neither DIDs nor ERC-8004 ([a2aproject/A2A specification.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/a2aproject/A2A/main/docs/specification.md)); Entra Agent ID is GA with agent objects, Conditional Access and ID Protection plans ([MicrosoftDocs whats-new-agent-id.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MicrosoftDocs/entra-docs/main/docs/agent-id/whats-new-agent-id.md)); NANDA Index v2 uses `urn:air:` URNs anchored to DNS with no blockchain mention ([projnanda/nanda-index-v2](https://github.com/projnanda/nanda-index-v2)); IETF WIMSE adopted draft-ietf-wimse-aims as a WG document in September 2026 ([ietf-wg-wimse/draft-ietf-wimse-aims](https://github.com/ietf-wg-wimse/draft-ietf-wimse-aims)). The exception is **Hashgraph Online's HCS-14 Universal Agent ID**, a draft standard whose `uaid:aid:` mode is a SHA-384 hash of canonical metadata and whose `uaid:did:` mode wraps an existing DID, creatable fully offline without Hedera ([hol.org HCS-14](https://hol.org/docs/standards/hcs-14/), snippet; SDK source at [hashgraph-online/standards-sdk](https://github.com/hashgraph-online/standards-sdk/tree/main/src/hcs-14)); HOL's public registry already serves profiles titled "ERC-8004 Agent #34880" under `uaid:aid:` identifiers ([hol.org registry](https://hol.org/registry/agent/uaid:aid:4x3ZybjoTeiNoipBvwMiJ48eTyD59dvo5TBKQ3CQKxeMPU36gC6yTegAdN91Who5vn)). A bureau should expect to maintain its own crosswalk from `(agentRegistry, agentId)` to these identifiers rather than find the agentId inside them.

## Payment rails settle value but ask nothing about the counterparty

**x402** is a bare HTTP-402 settlement rail. Protocol v2 (dated 2025-12-09) uses `PAYMENT-REQUIRED`, `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` and `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` headers — the `X-PAYMENT` header in the AgentBureau spec is **v1 terminology** — with EIP-3009 authorizations for the EVM `exact` scheme, CAIP-2 network ids (`eip155:8453` for Base), facilitator `/verify` (read-only) and `/settle` endpoints, and `exact`, `upto`, `batch-settlement` and `auth-capture` schemes ([x402 spec v2](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md); [README](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402)). The only payer datum in the spec is the wallet address in `authorization.from` (echoed as an optional `payer` in `/verify`/`/settle` responses); KYC, disputes, refunds and reputation appear nowhere, and the `bazaar` discovery extension carries only self-declared seller metadata ([bazaar.md](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/extensions/bazaar.md)). The spec's claim that "x402 asks nothing about the payer" is therefore exactly right. Governance moved to the `x402-foundation` GitHub org (coinbase/x402 is now a "development fork"); the repo's `TSC.md` lists Coinbase, Cloudflare and Stripe on the Technical Steering Committee and a Technical Charter dated 2026-03-31 ([TSC.md](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/TSC.md); [foundation folder](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/tree/main/foundation)). The Linux Foundation announced the x402 Foundation on 2026-04-02 naming Adyen, AWS, American Express, Google, Microsoft, Mastercard, Shopify, Stripe and Visa among ~22 initial supporters, and the operational launch with **40 members across three tiers** was reported on 2026-07-14 ([LF press release](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-is-launching-the-x402-foundation-and-welcoming-the-contribution-of-the-x402-protocol), snippet; [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320813/20260717/visa-mastercard-stripe-back-open-standard-letting-ai-agents-pay-autonomously.htm), snippet).

The spec's volume trio ("160M+ payments, ~129M on Base, $42M by mid-2026") was **not found verbatim anywhere**. The best-sourced anchors are Chainalysis's report that cumulative x402 transactions on Base rose from ≈1,500 in mid-2025 to **>138 million by Q3 2026**, with >87% of payments between $0.0001 and $0.10 ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/base-logs-138-million-x402-payments-as-ai-agent-revenue-draws-scrutiny/), snippet); a 280-day Base window of **136,708,672 transactions worth $44,121,383** ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-x402-payments-on-base-top-138m-as-ai-agents-pay-in-cents/), snippet); and an April 2026 all-chains figure of >165M transactions and ≈$50M ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/chainalysis-138m-base-x402-bot-payments-87-under-10-cents), snippet). Outliers run both ways — one explainer claims >$600M and 500,000 agent wallets, uncorroborated ([eco.com](https://eco.com/support/support/en/articles/14845485-coinbase-agentic-wallets), snippet) — and TRM Labs estimates **only 0.6%–7.5% of screened x402 commerce is genuinely agent-driven**, with only 246 of ≈37,292 services having ≥20 unique paying agents ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/base-logs-138-million-x402-payments-as-ai-agent-revenue-draws-scrutiny/), snippet). The right move is to cite Chainalysis and disclose TRM's caveat together; a reviewer who knows the second number will discount the first.

**Google AP2** reached v0.2.0 on 2026-04-28 ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)), and v0.2 **replaced the launch-era Intent/Cart/Payment mandate trio with Checkout and Payment mandates**, each in "open" (user-signed constraints) and "closed" (bound to one transaction, agent-signed) forms, secured as SD-JWTs; agent-facing open mandates must carry the agent's public key as a `cnf` claim ([AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)). Trust in an agent key "is provided by open Mandates that are signed by the User or a trust list of Agent Providers"; dispute retention and retrieval, agent-to-agent delegation and how a merchant decides to trust a shopping agent are explicitly out of scope ([AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)). The `a2a-x402` extension (spec v0.1, built with Coinbase, EF and MetaMask) exists and makes no identity or trust statements ([a2a-x402](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/a2a-x402)). The reported FIDO Alliance contribution is secondary-only ([MintMCP](https://www.mintmcp.com/blog/ap2-agent-payments-protocol), snippet). AP2 solves authorization provenance, not counterparty trust; the "trust list of Agent Providers" is the slot a bureau could populate.

**Visa TAP** recognizes agents by RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures verified against a Visa-run Agent Registry of public keys; merchants receive a binary verified-agent signal, an operation-bound intent (`agent-browser-auth` vs `agent-payer-auth`) and optionally consumer identifiers such as PARs ([visa/trusted-agent-protocol](https://github.com/visa/trusted-agent-protocol)). The public repo is a 6-commit sample with no registry onboarding, scoring, dispute or liability text; Visa's own late-2025 release described "hundreds of controlled, real-world" transactions and mainstream adoption targeted for 2026 ([Visa IR](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2025/Visa-and-Partners-Complete-Secure-AI-Transactions-Setting-the-Stage-for-Mainstream-Adoption-in-2026/default.aspx), snippet). **Mastercard** is the only rail that markets an agent *score*: its September 2026 trust-and-intelligence services pair Skyfire's KYA identity with intent, behavioral and fraud signals into an "Agent Detection Score" that is in U.S. testing and serves issuer authorization, "a network-side agent-awareness signal… not a full Know-Your-Agent product" ([Mastercard press release](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/september/new-trust-and-intelligence-services.html), snippet; [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/mastercard-agent-detection-score-asks-110827423.html), snippet). Visa, Mastercard and Ant International announced a cross-network KYA framework in September 2026 that links agents to validated operators and shares certification while keeping decisioning network-specific ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/2026/visa-mastercard-team-with-ant-know-your-agent-framework), snippet). Skyfire's role shows networks source KYA identity from a third party rather than build it — the plug-in shape a neutral bureau could reuse.

In the card-checkout protocols the platform vets merchants, the merchant stays merchant-of-record and absorbs chargebacks, and the agent is trusted because it is the platform's own first party. OpenAI/Stripe **ACP** is "beta", date-versioned (latest stable 2026-04-17), governed by OpenAI and Stripe as founding maintainers with a stated path to neutral foundation stewardship, and addresses neither disputes nor agent verification on its README ([ACP repo](https://github.com/agentic-commerce-protocol/agentic-commerce-protocol)). OpenAI reportedly retired ChatGPT's in-chat Instant Checkout in March 2026 with only around a dozen Shopify merchants ever live ([Laioutr](https://www.laioutr.com/en/blog/chatgpt-instant-checkout-merchant-adoption-agentic-readiness-2026), snippet). Google **UCP**'s primary repo lists Checkout, Identity Linking, Order and Payment Token Exchange capabilities and cites AP2 mandates as a security pattern, with no text on merchant of record, disputes or agent reputation ([UCP repo](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp)). **No source supports the spec's "hundreds of thousands" of UCP-checkout merchants**: coverage reports 20–26 endorsing organizations, early live merchants Lowe's, Michaels, Poshmark and Reebok, and a Merchant Center interest-form flow limited to eligible merchants in the US, Canada and Australia ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/2026/google-takes-a-step-toward-owning-the-checkout-with-ucp/), snippet; [Google Merchant Center help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16837055?hl=en), snippet). The large merchant numbers in circulation belong to other programs — Shopify's auto-enrolment of eligible U.S. merchants in Shop Pay agent checkout ("a million merchants" per headline) ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/shopify-turns-agent-checkout-default-124019261.html), snippet) and Amazon's Buy for Me listing 400,000+ outside merchants without opt-in ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/06/amazons-ai-shopping-tool-sparks-backlash-from-some-online-retailers.html), snippet). When a merchant does not trust an agent today the remedy is blocking and litigation: a federal judge granted Amazon a preliminary injunction barring Perplexity's Comet from shopping in password-protected Amazon areas ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/judge-blocks-perplexitys-ai-bot-from-shopping-on-amazon-in-early-test-of-agentic-commerce/), snippet).

The stablecoin stack has converged on wallet policy + x402 transport + chain settlement, with no layer assessing reliability. Circle launched Arc mainnet on 2026-09-16 and an "Agent Stack" (Agent Wallets with spend caps and allowlists, hosted x402 facilitator settling on Arc, Base and Polygon) ([Circle pressroom](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet), snippet; [CryptoNinjas](https://www.cryptoninjas.net/news/arc-launches-agentic-payments-with-usdc-across-3-chains-removing-gas-wallet-hassles/), snippet); Coinbase's Agentic Wallets (2026-02-11) give each agent an MPC wallet with keys in TEEs, session caps and gasless Base settlement ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-launches-crypto-wallets-built-ai-agents), snippet). Coverage of the buyer-agent ↔ merchant-agent trust decision, by rail: x402 none; AP2 authorization proof plus an opaque trust list; TAP binary registry membership; Agent Pay an issuer-side score; ACP/UCP platform gatekeeping; ERC-8004 an open, Sybil-prone, Draft feedback ledger. Nothing offers a neutral, cross-rail, bidirectional history with dispute outcomes.

## Every live reputation system defers Sybil resistance to someone else

The live on-chain agent-reputation systems all record feedback and push scoring and Sybil resistance off-chain. Virtuals ACP writes reviews to ERC-8004 and ranks agents by "aGDP" economic contribution — a pure volume ranking ([ACP changelog](https://whitepaper.virtuals.io/acp/acp-changelogs), snippet); Olas's Mech Marketplace checks nothing in the registration file and a cleanup PR removed an advertised `supportedTrust: reputation` because "no such system exists" ([mech-client PR #265](https://github.com/valory-xyz/mech-client/pull/265)); Recall Rank converts competition outcomes into leaderboards ([Messari](https://messari.io/report/recall-open-markets-for-ai), snippet). Concordium's Agent Registry binds each agent to an account anchored to a ZK-verified human, but **one account can hold 2,000 agents** ([Concordium](https://www.concordium.com/article/the-agent-registry-explained), snippet) — any per-agent score will be farmed at the agent layer unless tenure and volume are attributed to the operator. Proof-of-personhood is the only widely deployed Sybil primitive (World ID 4.0 at "nearly 18 million" Orb-verified people; Human Passport at 2M+ accounts with a recommended threshold of 20 that its own docs say does not eliminate all Sybils) and it binds humans, not agents ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260417530721/en/The-New-World-ID-Proof-of-Human-for-the-AI-Era-Scales-Across-the-Digital-Platforms-People-and-Businesses-Use-Every-Day), snippet; [Passport docs](https://docs.passport.human.tech/building-with-passport/stamps/major-concepts/scoring-thresholds), snippet). OpenRank/EigenTrust remains the reference asymmetric design (pre-trust seeds, alpha default 0.5) ([Karma3Labs/openrank-sdk](https://github.com/Karma3Labs/openrank-sdk)), but one aggregator reports Karma3 Labs announced a wind-down in June 2026 — unverified, single source ([Lucidity Insights](https://lucidityinsights.com/infobytes/karma3-openrank-protocol), snippet).

KYA products split three ways and none publishes a reproducible open score. Rail attestations bind a request to an operator and consenting user via signatures or delegation-chain JWTs: Skyfire's KYAPay token names operator, user delegation and scoped spend ([Skyfire](https://skyfire.xyz/industry-articles/how-ai-agents-prove-identity-kya-authentication-authorization-payments/), snippet); Trulioo's Digital Agent Passport does KYB/KYC on the developer, locks code integrity and captures user consent ([Trulioo](https://www.trulioo.com/company/newsroom/company-news/trulioo-joins-google-ap2-trusted-agent-payments), snippet); Experian Agent Trust (April 2026) binds consumer, device and agent and keeps "behavior-based trust scores" in a closed registry ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260430719198/en/Experian-Announces-Agent-Trust-to-Power-Trusted-AI-Driven-Commerce), snippet); Sumsub's Agent-to-Human Binding (January 2026) and KYA Module for MCP (2026-10-19) tie agents to verified humans ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sumsubs-ai-agent-verification-introduces-agent-to-human-binding-to-establish-human-accountability-in-ai-302673467.html), snippet; [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/sumsub-expands-know-agent-putting-100000150.html), snippet). Enterprise vendors (Keycard, Natoma, SGNL, Descope, Arcade) bind an agent to a runtime or sponsoring user inside one organization ([Keycard](https://www.keycard.ai/blog/agent-security-stack/), snippet). The useful abstraction for AgentBureau's KYA tier is an ordinal over binding strength: none < self-signed key < directory-listed signer < KYB'd operator with delegation chain < KYB'd operator plus attested runtime. All of these verify authority to act, not past conduct. Arkose Labs reports that **only 2.3% of legitimate agent sessions carried a verified Web Bot Auth signature** in Q3 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/97-7-of-legitimate-ai-agent-sessions-carry-no-verified-id-arkose-finds/), snippet), so a score that depends on signed agents alone would cover almost nothing today.

The academic literature constrains the draft formula (score = 1000 × (0.25·Tenure + 0.30·VerifiedVolume + 0.25·DisputeRecord + 0.10·Stake + 0.10·KYAtier) − penalties) in four specific ways. Cheng & Friedman's sybilproofness result holds that **any symmetric reputation function is manipulable by Sybils** and only asymmetric flow/path-based functions resist — and a weighted sum of per-agent tenure, volume and disputes is symmetric ([Stanford seminar abstract](https://or.stanford.edu/seminars/friedman_111605.html), snippet). Volume is the most-faked signal in every market measured: wash trading averaged **>70% of reported volume on unregulated crypto exchanges** ([NBER w30783](https://nber.org/papers/w30783), snippet), NFT wash trading is detected by per-token seller→…→same-seller cycles and <30-day repurchases ([arXiv 2305.01543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01543), snippet), and the only agent registry measured is majority-Sybil. Tenure is a traded commodity — aged Amazon buyer accounts sell for $50–$500 and detection keys on dormant-then-burst patterns ([Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/account-aging), snippet) — while Friedman & Resnick show the only sustainable convention under cheap identities is that newcomers "pay their dues", with entry fees (stake) as the alternative ([Friedman & Resnick 2001](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/mechanism-design/2001-friedman.pdf), snippet). Stake as a fixed 10% weight cannot deliver the "farming costs ≥10× the unlocked limit" target because, per "Tempting the Agent" (motivated explicitly by ERC-8004, ERC-8183 and x402), **accumulated reputation raises the one-shot cheating payoff** and "targeted staking" is what removes the temptation ([arXiv 2609.02992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02992), snippet); the restaking literature's cost-of-corruption framing analyzes buffers of only ~1.1×, so a 10× target is conservative but only if "cost" counts slashable stake and genuinely transferred value, not points ([arXiv 2407.21785](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21785v1), snippet). A single global score is launderable across order classes: an attacker with evidence on one cheap skill "can take over routing for the cost of one fabricated episode" ([arXiv 2606.14200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14200), snippet). What has held up empirically is outcome-based quality (an eBay 2026 field experiment on a seller measure that predicts poor outcomes shifted buyers to better sellers without hurting conversion) ([Springer QME 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11129-026-09313-1), snippet), bilateral-blind feedback (Airbnb's simultaneous reveal cut retaliation) ([Fradkin et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bias-and-Reciprocity-in-Online-Reviews:-Evidence-on-Fradkin-Grewal/159485ce42f0462f3f01d6d732cf1e5452588f72), snippet), and time-decayed Beta models with confidence shrinkage (Cairn uses a three-day half-life; Storj tunes a geometric forgetting factor of 0.987) ([arXiv 2609.19502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19502), snippet; [Storj forum](https://forum.storj.io/t/tuning-audit-scoring/14084/16), snippet). DisputeRecord is the best-supported term, provided disputes are adjudicated outcomes rather than ratings.

Regulatory analogies all key on natural persons. The CJEU's SCHUFA ruling makes a credit score itself an Article 22 automated decision when "decisively" relied on ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-rules-that-a-credit-score-constitutes-automated-decision-making-under-the-gdpr), snippet); FCRA's "consumer report" reaches "character, general reputation, or personal characteristics" used for eligibility ([Wiley](https://www.wileyconnect.com/ftc-consumer-protection-and-privacy-enforcement-series-the-fair-credit-reporting-act-who-is-covered-and-how-to-comply), snippet); the EU AI Act Article 5(1)(c) social-scoring ban reaches private actors and is triggered especially by aggregating "multiple unrelated social contexts" ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-the-eu-ai-act-unpacking-social-scoring-as-a-prohibited-ai-practice), snippet). No regulator has addressed scoring AI agents or their operators. Scoring the KYB'd operator entity and the agent key, with human-binding kept as a boolean attestation rather than an input to the number, keeps the bureau outside these regimes in most cases.

## ERC-8183 makes one evaluator final; Kleros is the only live human court with an agent on-ramp

ERC-8183 "Agentic Commerce" is a **Draft** ERC (created 2026-02-25, merged 2026-03-05) by Davide Crapis of the EF and three Virtuals engineers, defining a six-state job escrow — Open, Funded, Submitted, Completed, Rejected, Expired — in which a single per-job `evaluator` address, set at creation, alone may `complete` or `reject` a Submitted job; the Security Considerations state verbatim that the evaluator "is trusted once a job is Submitted and can complete or reject arbitrarily" and that there is **"No dispute resolution or arbitration; reject/expire is final"** ([ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md); [PR #1581](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1581)). The `IACPHook` interface offers `beforeAction`/`afterAction` on six functions, with `claimRefund` deliberately non-hookable so a reverting hook cannot trap funds past expiry; it is an escrow-lifecycle callback, not a wallet-authorization primitive ([ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)). The spec's own embedded reference code diverges from its normative text (reference `fund` lacks the `expectedBudget` front-running check, restricts `setBudget` to the provider, and allows `submit` from Open), so a fork must pick a side on each discrepancy.

The spec's "reference escrows on Base/Arbitrum/Arc" claim overstates what exists. The official `erc-8183/base-contracts` repository lists **"Deployments: None listed"** and **"Audits: None mentioned"**, and carries an open issue with seven automated "HIGH" findings (including "evaluators are paid per evaluation regardless of quality") and another reporting a UUPS storage-layout collision in a BSC fork that locked a real user's 19.8 USDT, both without maintainer reply ([erc-8183/base-contracts](https://github.com/erc-8183/base-contracts); [issue #12](https://github.com/erc-8183/base-contracts/issues/12); [issue #22](https://github.com/erc-8183/base-contracts/issues/22)). What is deployed: on Base, ClawWork's fork `erc8183-reference` (`ACPCore.sol` at `0x16213AB6a660A24f36d4F8DdACA7a3d0856A8AF5`, self-described as "production-proven" with "20,000+ AI agents", no audit named) ([erc8183/erc8183-reference](https://github.com/erc8183/erc8183-reference)); on Arbitrum, **PactEscrow v2** at `0x220B97972d6028Acd70221890771E275e7734BFB`, an immutable, non-conforming contract using creator-approval-or-arbitrator release with timeout auto-release, reporting 20 settlements, filed by a self-described autonomous agent ([issue #25](https://github.com/erc-8183/base-contracts/issues/25)); on Arc, a testnet-only reference at `0x0747EEf0706327138c69792bF28Cd525089e4583` ([Arc tutorial](https://docs.arc.io/arc/tutorials/create-your-first-erc-8183-job), snippet); and on BSC mainnet, BNB Chain's **APEX**, whose `EvaluatorRouterUpgradeable` acts as both evaluator and hook and whose immutable `OptimisticPolicy` auto-approves on silence and routes a client dispute to a whitelisted-voter quorum ([bnb-chain/apex-contracts](https://github.com/bnb-chain/apex-contracts); [compliance doc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bnb-chain/apex-contracts/main/docs/erc-8183-compliance.md)). "ArcBounty" was not found under that name; the closest match is the Sofiia7/ARC bounty board (0 stars, ERC-8183 + ERC-8004, "on Monad, Base, and Arc") ([GitHub topic erc-8183](https://github.com/topics/erc-8183)). Virtuals ACP v2 lets the buyer name itself evaluator by default and documents no dispute or appeal; Fundstrat reports ≈$4.5M cumulative gross ACP fees and completed jobs peaking near 414,000 in January 2026 ([Virtual-Protocol/acp-node-v2](https://github.com/Virtual-Protocol/acp-node-v2); [Fundstrat Q2](https://fundstrat.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/20260624-Digital-Asset-Strategy-Virtuals-Protocol-–-Growing-Agentic-GDP-Q2-Update.pdf), snippet). Every live system resolves disagreement by one of three primitives — a pre-named evaluator's final verdict, optimistic auto-approve with a small veto set, or timeout auto-release — and none has AgentBureau's rules → bonded human review → bonded appeal ladder, which makes it differentiated and also means no field data exists on how agents behave under bonded appeal.

Kleros V2 on Arbitrum One is the reusable human-juror tier. It created an **Agentic Commerce Court (court 34)** in August 2026 with minStake 11,000 PNK, **feeForJuror 0.00027 ETH**, hidden votes and a court jump to the human Commerce Court (0.018 ETH/juror) at 7 jurors; its Dispute Service exposes a hand-keyed MCP/JSON-RPC endpoint with evidence/commit/vote periods of 10/45/30 minutes and a 36-hour appeal period, so an agent can file without deploying an arbitrable ([court hierarchy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kleros/docs/main/court/court-hierarchy.mdx); [dispute flow](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kleros/docs/main/ai-tools/dispute-service/dispute-flow.mdx)). Appeals draw 2n+1 jurors, the challenger pays 2× the appeal cost and the winner 1×, and if only one side funds it wins by default ([appeals](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kleros/docs/main/court/appeals.mdx)). A first round at three jurors costs 0.00081 ETH and a first appeal 0.00378 ETH for the challenger; at the human-court jump a 15-juror round is ≈0.54 ETH for the challenger — an AI→human escalation that mirrors the bureau's design and could serve as the appeal of last resort above the ≤$200 tier. Scale is the risk: the V2 dashboard showed 45 active jurors and 289 cases all-time ([v2.kleros.builders](https://v2.kleros.builders/), snippet), and a 2020 paper notes "there is no empirical study of the error rate of Kleros" ([arXiv 2008.10326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10326), snippet). UMA's optimistic oracle (2-hour default liveness, symmetric bonds, 48-hour DVM vote) is the fastest challenge-window pattern but its DVM votes were found concentrated in ten wallets ([UMA DVM interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UMAprotocol/docs/master/docs/oracle/dvm-interface.md); [Laika Labs](https://laikalabs.ai/prediction-markets/polymarket-disputed-markets-uma-oracle-resolution), snippet). Upwork's "only the paying side wins" rule — with forum-reported fees of $291–$337.50 on $100 claims — is the operating precedent for bonded appeal and a warning on fee-to-claim ratio ([Upwork help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/14044146250259), snippet). Card-network anchors for auto-rules: 120-day Visa filing windows (540 for pre-orders), eBay's $750 signature-confirmation threshold, and Visa's April 2026 VAMP tier adding an $8 per-dispute fee above a 1.5% ratio ([Chargeflow](https://chargeflow.io/blog/visa-chargeback-dispute-rules-fees-time-limit), snippet; [eBay policy](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/signature-confirmation%20policy?id=5154), snippet; [Chargeback.io](https://www.chargeback.io/blog/visa-chargeback-guide-for-merchants), snippet). For delivery evidence, zkTLS (Reclaim proxy-witness, TLSNotary MPC, Opacity) is the only general way to prove a carrier page without API access — a 2026 formal model (zkPoPE) shows Reclaim attests a 1200 kB response in 7.28 s on a mid-range phone ([ePrint 2026/2187](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2187), snippet) — but no shipment-tracking templates or production escrow consuming carrier webhooks were found.

Legal exposure clusters around the evaluator key. Under FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance the hinge is "total independent control" over value ([FinCEN CVC guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf), snippet); a bureau holding a key that unilaterally directs escrowed funds to either party sits closer to that line than a non-custodial contract, though no ruling on the pattern exists. California's DFPI licenses "internet escrow agents" (bond ≥$25,000) even for out-of-state operators serving California consumers, and Escrow.com's license table shows Alabama and Colorado classify online escrow as money transmission ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/escrow-law/about-the-escrow-law/), snippet; [Escrow.com](https://www.escrow.com/escrow-licenses), snippet). A Council of the EU working document treats AI in ADR as potentially high-risk under AI Act Annex III(8)(a) and says automated tools should "help and assist the natural persons in charge of ADR, but not replace them" ([Council WK 7931/2025](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-7931-2025-INIT/en/pdf), snippet). Taiwan's Virtual Asset Service Act (passed 2026-06-30, effective Q1 2027 at earliest) introduces seven permission-based categories including custody, with up to seven years' imprisonment for unlicensed operation ([TRM Labs](https://trmlabs.com/resources/blog/unpacking-taiwans-virtual-asset-service-act-what-crypto-and-stablecoin-issuers-need-to-know), snippet).

## EAS is confirmed at the Base predeploy; TEE stacks bind keys, not models

The EAS predeploy claim is **verified for Base mainnet**: the eas-contracts README lists Base v1.0.1 with EAS at `0x4200000000000000000000000000000000000021` and SchemaRegistry at `0x4200000000000000000000000000000000000020` (plus a v1.2.0 EIP712Proxy and Indexer at other addresses), the OP Stack specification names both as Bedrock predeploys, and Coinbase's verifications repo cites the same pair for Base Mainnet ([eas-contracts README](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts); [OP predeploys.md](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/blob/main/specs/protocol/predeploys.md); [coinbase/verifications](https://github.com/coinbase/verifications)). Base Sepolia is strongly implied by OP-Stack genesis convention and by Coinbase listing Base Sepolia attester and indexer addresses, but no fetched page printed the Base Sepolia EAS address itself. Ethereum mainnet and Arbitrum One run the older v0.26 contract at non-predeploy addresses. Three implementation details matter. The contract source is `Semver(1, 4, 0)` with EIP-712 domain `"EAS"/"1.4.0"`, but the Base predeploy is v1.0.1, so off-chain attestations for Base must be signed against the deployed version string — confirm with `eas.getVersion()` ([EAS.sol](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts/blob/master/contracts/EAS.sol); [eas-sdk README](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-sdk)). Schema resolvers execute only inside on-chain `_attest`/`_revoke`; off-chain attestations never touch them, so "attester == bureau" for off-chain claims must be enforced by verifiers checking the EIP-712 signer ([SchemaResolver.sol](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts/blob/master/contracts/resolver/SchemaResolver.sol); the `AttesterResolver` example is the exact pattern — [AttesterResolver.sol](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts/blob/master/contracts/resolver/examples/AttesterResolver.sol)). Off-chain revocation is `revokeOffchain(bytes32 uid)` keyed by `(msg.sender, uid)`, read via `getRevokeOffchain(revoker, uid)`, with no reason field; EAS's own docs recommend issuing a new attestation whose `refUID` points at the revoked one to carry history ([IEAS.sol](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts/blob/master/contracts/IEAS.sol); [revocation.md](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-docs-site/blob/main/docs/core--concepts/revocation.md)). Coinbase Verifications — Verified Account, Verified Country and Coinbase One schemas on Base, gated so "only permitted Coinbase attesters can use these schemas" via resolver `0xD867…` — is the production precedent closest to the bureau's attester-gated design and a usable operator-KYC input, though point-in-time with no revocation SLA ([coinbase/verifications](https://github.com/coinbase/verifications)). Verax (Consensys, deployed on Base with an EAS-compatibility `AttestationReader`) and Sign Protocol are the nearest alternatives; EAS's ZK playbook calls W3C VCs "not recommended" for smart-contract use and recommends Merkle-root private-data attestations plus SP1/RISC Zero proofs over off-chain attestations ([linea-attestation-registry](https://github.com/Consensys/linea-attestation-registry); [recommended-tooling.md](https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-docs-site/blob/main/docs/zk--playbook/recommended-tooling.md)). On privacy, the EDPB's finalised Guidelines 02/2025 treat hashes of personal data as still personal data, so an "orphaned hash" of operator identity on-chain is a contested defence, not a safe harbour ([INATBA response](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/webform/public_consultation_reply/inatba-response_edpb-guidelines-2025-06-09.pdf), snippet).

Every production "verifiable agent" stack found is TEE-based and binds a *key*, not a model. Phala's dstack (a Linux Foundation CCC project, Intel TDX, audited by zkSecurity) derives per-app keys from a KMS governed by on-chain `DstackKms`/`DstackApp` contracts whose allowed compose hashes define the app, with app ID equal to the DstackApp contract address ([Dstack-TEE/dstack](https://github.com/Dstack-TEE/dstack); [onchain-governance.md](https://github.com/Dstack-TEE/dstack/blob/next/docs/onchain-governance.md)). Phala's ERC-8004 TEE agent template derives the agent's Ethereum address inside the CVM, places it zero-padded in the quote's `report_data`, and mints the agentId from that address; **the agentId itself is not inside the quote** — the binding is address-based ([tee_auth.py](https://github.com/Phala-Network/erc-8004-tee-agent/blob/main/src/agent/tee_auth.py)). Flashbots' Flashtestations stores the TEE address from `reportData[0:20]` and a hash of extended data from `reportData[20:52]` and keeps governance-approved WorkloadIds ([flashbots/flashtestations](https://github.com/flashbots/flashtestations)). On-chain DCAP verification via Automata costs ~4–5M gas, or ~0.5M with a RISC Zero/SP1 Groth16 wrapper ([automata-dcap-attestation](https://github.com/automata-network/automata-dcap-attestation)); AWS Nitro documents are COSE_Sign1 with PCRs and an optional `user_data`/`nonce` ([nsm-api attestation_process.md](https://github.com/aws/aws-nitro-enclaves-nsm-api/blob/main/docs/attestation_process.md)). None of these attests model weights served by a third-party API or LLM behaviour; "Proof-of-Guardrail" attestation proves only that a guardrail configuration was loaded ([arXiv 2603.05786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05786), snippet). MCP's 2026-07-28 revision deprecated RFC 7591 dynamic registration in favour of Client ID Metadata Documents (HTTPS-URL client ids) and added issuer validation; neither MCP nor A2A carries a native attestation object, and the MCP registry entries are namespace-verified, not signed ([2026-07-28 changelog](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/blob/main/docs/specification/2026-07-28/changelog.mdx); [modelcontextprotocol/registry](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry)).

## Wallet primitives are production-ready on Base; prompt injection is the operational attack

The "cold owner key + hot policy-bound agent key" design can be built from audited, Base-deployed contracts today. EIP-7702 is Final and live since Pectra (2025-05-07) ([EIP-7702](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-7702.md); [EIP-7600](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-7600.md)); ERC-4337 is Final; ERC-7579 is Draft but implemented by Kernel, Safe7579 and MetaMask DeleGator ([ERC-7579](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-7579.md)). Coinbase's `SpendPermissionManager` (`0xf85210B21cC50302F477BA56686d2019dC9b67Ad`, audited three times by Spearbit/Cantina) expresses one token × allowance-per-period × [start, end), revocable by owner or spender, with no target allowlist — the spender contract must enforce where funds go ([coinbase/spend-permissions](https://github.com/coinbase/spend-permissions); [SpendPermissionManager.sol](https://github.com/coinbase/spend-permissions/blob/main/src/SpendPermissionManager.sol)). MetaMask's Delegation Framework v1.3.0 is on Base mainnet (`DelegationManager 0xdb9B1e94B5b69Df7e401DDbedE43491141047dB3`) with composable caveat enforcers for allowed targets/methods, ERC-20 period transfer, streaming, timestamps, limited calls and nonces ([Deployments.md](https://github.com/MetaMask/delegation-framework/blob/main/documents/Deployments.md); [src/enforcers](https://github.com/MetaMask/delegation-framework/tree/main/src/enforcers)). Zodiac Roles Modifier is on Base at `0xF2964CE6161ce0e75964Fe7927cE114cb0B283D5` with a Roles V3 audit dated October 2026 ([zodiac-modifier-roles](https://github.com/gnosisguild/zodiac-modifier-roles)). The RPC-level permission standards ERC-7715/ERC-7710 remain Draft, define no revocation method or rule vocabulary, and MetaMask's implementation is Flask-only ([ERC-7715](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-7715.md); [ERC-7710](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-7710.md)). Hosted agent wallets converge on keys in a TEE plus a server-side deny-by-default rule list — CDP rejects any request no rule matches, Turnkey evaluates root quorum → explicit deny → any allow → implicit deny — while AgentKit itself "does not gate transfers behind human approval, enforce spend caps, or allowlist destinations" ([CDP policy engine](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/wallets/security-and-policies/policy-engine/overview.md), snippet; [tkhq/docs policy-engine.mdx](https://github.com/tkhq/docs/blob/main/snippets/shared/policy-engine.mdx); [coinbase/agentkit](https://github.com/coinbase/agentkit)). For the bureau signer, AWS and GCP KMS both support secp256k1; the robust rotation pattern is to make the verifier-facing identity a contract (Safe or an OpenZeppelin `MultiSignerERC7913` signer) so attestation verification survives key rotation ([ERC-1271](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-1271.md); [openzeppelin-contracts releases](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/openzeppelin-contracts/releases)).

The attacks that matter are pre-authorization manipulations of what gets signed. Unit 42 documented a deals-aggregator site whose hidden payload makes a UCP shopping agent append a **$100 gift card to the Cart Mandate** with an attacker recipient ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/retail-fraud-agentic-ai/), snippet); "Whispers of Wealth" showed product metadata can hijack an AP2 agent's ranking and exfiltrate user data ([arXiv 2601.22569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22569v1), snippet), and a follow-up notes AP2 signs transaction data *after* authorization, leaving everything that shaped the cart unprotected ([emergentmind listing](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.23858), snippet). Anthropic measured a **23.6% attack success rate against a browser agent, 11.2% after mitigations**, and 0% on four browser-specific classes, with purchases requiring confirmation and financial sites blocked by default ([Claude for Chrome](https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-chrome)). MCP tool poisoning and "rug pulls" (tool descriptions changed after approval) are catalogued as OWASP MCP03, with CVE-2025-54136 as a real instance ([OWASP MCP Top 10](https://github.com/OWASP/www-project-mcp-top-10); [SecureW2](https://securew2.com/?p=72938), snippet). On-chain spending caps bound loss per period but do not stop paying the wrong merchant for the wrong item, so the policy engine needs semantic checks — merchant allowlists and cart-hash binding between what was approved and what is signed. For indexing on Base, the OP Stack derivation spec says the `safe` head "may only be reorged out due to L1 reorgs" and the `finalized` head follows finalized L1 (~13 minutes), and any user can force a transaction through `OptimismPortal.depositTransaction` within a 12-hour sequencing window ([derivation.md](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/blob/main/specs/protocol/derivation.md); [forced-transaction.mdx](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/docs/blob/main/pages/stack/transactions/forced-transaction.mdx)). The toolchain is current: Foundry v1.8.0 adds `forge test --symbolic` and `--mutate`, Slither 0.11.6 supports solc 0.8.35, and OpenZeppelin's proxy guidance "is now shifting towards UUPS proxies" ([Foundry v1.8.0](https://github.com/foundry-rs/foundry/releases/tag/v1.8.0); [crytic/slither releases](https://github.com/crytic/slither/releases); [OZ proxy README](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/openzeppelin-contracts/blob/master/contracts/proxy/README.adoc)). Audit quotes for a small system cluster at $15,000–$40,000 plus $5,000–$20,000 per re-audit round ([Sherlock](https://sherlock.xyz/post/smart-contract-audit-pricing-a-market-reference-for-2026), snippet).

## The bureau seat is empty but ringed by rails, oracles and insurers

No entity found combines operator KYA, a trust score, escrow evaluation and dispute resolution. The field splits into KYA/identity vendors tied to a rail or compliance product (Skyfire with ~$9.5M seed and integrations into Forter and Mastercard; Sumsub; Trulioo inside AP2; Concordium's CIS-8004 registry, selected as XDC's exclusive identity layer on 2026-10-15; Catena Labs with $30M Series A and a reported OCC preliminary approval; World AgentKit binding World ID to x402 agents) ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/skyfire/__-gSNwLdAbLR2EH3jQO5ja24BZ2dqjmKWC_BS3-4pf1s), snippet; [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/10/15/xdc-network-selects-concordium-as-identity-verification-layer-for-ai-agents/), snippet; [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/catena-labs-30m-series-a-bank-charter/), snippet; [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/03/17/sam-altman-s-world-teams-up-with-coinbase-to-prove-there-is-a-real-person-behind-every-ai-transaction), snippet); a long tail of mostly single-developer ERC-8004 reputation oracles on Base with incompatible scales — RNWY (scores 50–95, four Sybil signals, ES256 attestations, `RNWYTrustOracle` on Base, free API, no company information) ([rnwy/sdk](https://github.com/rnwy/sdk)), 8k4's IGGY-Score sold per query over x402, Helixa's token-gated 0–100 Cred Score, DJD, MainStreet, SENTINEL, Verity, Assay, AXIS's AAA–D "C-Score", and mnemopay's 300–850 "Agent Credit Score" ([awesome-erc8004](https://github.com/sudeepb02/awesome-erc8004); [awesome-agent-trust](https://github.com/CodeSigils/awesome-agent-trust)); and escrow/dispute efforts that disclaim scoring — ERC-8183/Virtuals ACP, Synmerco (escrow + disputes + insurance on Base), Internet Court (a 27-firm coalition including OKX, MetaMask and Matter Labs announced 2026-07-10) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/07/10/okx-metamask-matter-labs-back-dispute-resolution-court-for-ai-agents), snippet). Platforms have converged on authentication and now detection, not counterparty reputation. Highest-probability entrants into the seat: Coinbase/x402 Foundation, Mastercard/Visa, and Catena.

## Design implications for AgentBureau

**Identity keying and transfer handling.** Key every bureau file on `(agentRegistry, agentId)`, pin the ABI you index, watch `Upgraded` events on the upgradeable singletons, and treat the ERC-721 `Transfer(from, to, tokenId)` event as a mandatory "new account holder" input: version the record at each transfer, re-check `agentWallet`, the registration file and any ENSIP-25 record, and discount pre-transfer history unless operator or wallet continuity is shown. Treat the Validation Registry as absent on mainnet; if the bureau later wants to act as a validator, ERC-8294's operator-diverse validator-network interface is the thread to follow. Build a crosswalk table to HCS-14 UAIDs, did:pkh of the token owner, Web Bot Auth key thumbprints and A2A card signing keys rather than expecting an agentId inside them; the cleanest cross-check is an A2A Agent Card field carrying the bureau's EAS UID and agentId so the card key, the attested TEE key and the on-chain `agentWallet` can be reconciled.

**Rebuild the score around what the literature says survives.** Drop raw ERC-8004 feedback as a direct input (59–91% Sybil on every measured chain; the bureau *can* write feedback without consent, but its own entries would be one voice in that noise — publish them as a signal to others, not as an input to itself). Count volume only from settled escrow outcomes with counterparties whose own score clears a floor, net out A→B→A cycles and short-interval repurchases, cap any single counterparty cluster's contribution, and compute the term asymmetrically from a published pre-trusted seed set (EigenTrust-style) so a Sybil cannot raise it by creating identities that interact with it. Replace calendar tenure with *active* tenure that saturates quickly and triggers holds on dormant-then-burst patterns. Make stake a band-conditional gate — slashable stake ≥ k × the order limit the band unlocks — rather than a 10% weight, because the exit-scam payoff scales with the limit. Attribute DisputeRecord and penalties to the KYB'd operator entity and propagate to all its agents (Concordium's 2,000-agents-per-account precedent shows why), while tenure and volume accrue per agent. Scope bands per order class so cheap-skill evidence cannot unlock high-value categories. Keep feedback bilateral-blind, use a time-decayed Beta model with confidence shrinkage toward the prior, and count only slashable stake and genuinely transferred value in the 10× attack-cost accounting. Score the operator and the agent key; keep human binding as a boolean attestation outside the number; and ship FCRA-style dispute/correction, adverse-action explanations and permissible-purpose access control regardless of legal trigger, because counterparties will demand them.

**Attestations and signing.** Issue off-chain EAS attestations by default signed against the Base predeploy's deployed EIP-712 version (1.0.1, confirm with `getVersion()`), publish band changes on-chain through a schema whose resolver follows the `AttesterResolver` pattern, record reason codes by issuing a successor attestation with `refUID` to the revoked UID, and have verifiers check both the EIP-712 signer and `getRevokeOffchain(bureau, uid)`. Make the canonical attester a contract (Safe with KMS-backed owners, later a threshold signer) so key rotation never changes what verifiers check, and publish `(keyId, address, validFrom, validUntil)` in the registry if raw EOAs are ever used. For KYA tier 3, verify TEE quotes off-chain (dcap-qvl, dstack-verifier, NRAS) and publish the result as an EAS attestation carrying agentId, measurement hash, quote hash, policy version and expiry; require a reproducible build recipe and a published expected-measurement set; score a code-hash-only (SLSA/in-toto) tier lower; and be explicit that no tier attests third-party model weights or LLM behaviour. Keep operator PII off-chain and treat salted hashes as contested under the EDPB guidelines.

**Escrow, evaluation and disputes.** Fork from the ERC-8183 spec text plus `erc-8183/base-contracts`, deciding explicitly on `expectedBudget`, the `setBudget` caller and submit-from-Open, and budget a fresh audit (no audited reference exists). APEX's router-as-evaluator with an immutable optimistic policy is the closest on-chain analogue of the bureau's auto-rule tier and worth studying for its deviation log. Design the evaluator so it can only choose between the two parties, never a third address, with `claimRefund` permissionless — the strongest available non-custodial argument under FinCEN's "total independent control" test — and plan for state escrow licensing (California's internet-escrow regime at minimum) and Taiwan's VASA custody category from 2027. Use Kleros's Agentic Commerce Court as the appeal of last resort above the in-house ≤$200 tier, adopt the "only the paying side wins" default from Upwork and Kleros fee timeouts, and set the 2% bond with a floor and cap so a $200 dispute posts more than $4 but never approaches Upwork's claim-exceeding fees. Build carrier-tracking evidence server-side and author Reclaim/Opacity providers for USPS/UPS/FedEx pages yourself; none exist. Pay evaluators and reviewers in a way linked to ruling quality, since per-ruling fees are flagged as distorting. Log dispute rates per bond level from day one: no field evidence exists on whether bonds suppress legitimate agent disputes, and the bureau can generate it.

**Wallet authority.** Put the hot-key spending cap at the account layer — a Coinbase Spend Permission to a bureau-controlled spender contract that enforces destinations, or a DeleGator/Kernel session key with period-transfer plus allowed-target caveats — and use ERC-8183 hooks, if at all, only to additionally check that funded Jobs match policy; the hook is an escrow callback, not a signing-authority primitive. Enforce deny-by-default policy inside the signer (CDP/Turnkey-style or self-hosted KMS behind a policy service) with semantic checks (merchant allowlist, cart-hash binding, per-merchant caps), pin MCP tool definitions by hash and re-approve on change, treat every fetched page as untrusted, and confirm purchases above a per-transaction threshold. Expect to write and audit a small "ERC-721-holder-is-owner" validator module, since none exists off the shelf. Index Base by `safe`/`finalized` block tags rather than confirmation counts, and make revocation and pause callable from an L1-originated deposit so sequencer censorship cannot block them.

**Positioning and plug-in points.** The honest "why us" is rail- and platform-neutrality plus packaging the four functions that fraud vendors and PSPs currently assemble from separate suppliers. The concrete integration surfaces are an x402 extension or facilitator `/verify` enrichment (the response already returns `payer`), a signer on AP2's "trust list of Agent Providers", a data source into TAP registry metadata or Mastercard's detection inputs the way Skyfire is, a Radar-style signal travelling with Stripe SPTs, and a feed into Forter/Riskified decisions. Join the W3C Agent Identity CG, the AAIF Identity & Trust WG and the ERC-8004 best-practices process early, because the "trust level" definitions the bureau will be measured against are being drafted now. Finally, fix stale terminology in the spec: `X-PAYMENT` → `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`/`PAYMENT-REQUIRED`; AP2 "Intent/Cart/Payment mandates" → v0.2 Checkout and Payment mandates (open/closed); and cite Chainalysis's 138M-on-Base figure with TRM's 0.6–7.5% caveat in place of the uncorroborated trio.

## Conclusion

The research changes the AgentBureau question from "is there room?" to "what counts as evidence?" The room is unambiguous — ERC-8004's own authors delegate aggregation, Sybil filtering and insurer/auditor roles to third parties, ERC-8183 declares disputes out of scope, and every rail stops at authentication — but the two data sources the spec leans on, ERC-8004 feedback and x402 volume, are precisely the ones measurement has found to be mostly noise (59–91% Sybil reviewers; 0.6–7.5% genuinely agent-driven payments). A bureau that ingests them as-is would inherit their credibility problem. The defensible product is therefore narrower and harder than the spec implies: a score whose inputs are escrow-settled outcomes the bureau itself evaluated, operator-level KYB, and band-scaled slashable stake, published as attestations that rails and fraud vendors consume the way they already consume Skyfire's identity tokens.

Two things to settle before the next design revision. First, the one-evaluator finality of ERC-8183 is both the bureau's opening and its legal exposure: whoever holds the evaluator key is closest to "total independent control" over escrowed funds, so the contract should constrain that key to choosing between the two parties and the business should budget for state escrow licensing. Second, the evidence base is thin in a way that will not fix itself — the proxy block means nearly every adoption figure here is a snippet, no empirical study exists of Kleros error rates, bonded-appeal behavior or tenure farming in agent markets, and Base's fee economics were not examined at all. AgentBureau will be among the first systems able to generate that data; instrumenting dispute rates, Sybil-cluster detections and transfer-triggered re-scores from launch is worth more than any number this report could cite.
