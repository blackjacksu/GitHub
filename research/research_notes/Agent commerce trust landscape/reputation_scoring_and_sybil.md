# Reputation scoring, KYA products and sybil resistance for transacting AI agents (as of 2026-10-21)

Scope note: all findings below come from web search result snippets and summaries gathered on 2026-10-21. The research sandbox blocked direct fetches of arxiv.org, eips.ethereum.org, tiger-research.com and most vendor sites (proxy policy denial), so no full-text papers or specs were read; every quoted figure is as it appeared in search snippets and should be checked against the primary document before being load-bearing. Peer-review status is flagged per item: [peer-reviewed], [preprint], [vendor claim], [secondary]. Dates are the publication dates reported by the sources.

## Key question 1: On-chain and decentralized reputation systems (EigenTrust/OpenRank, Human Passport, World ID, PoH, BrightID, EAS, ERC-8004, Farcaster/Neynar, agent-specific projects)

### Takeaway
Every live on-chain agent-reputation system found (ERC-8004, Virtuals ACP, Olas Mech Marketplace, Recall) records feedback but pushes scoring, aggregation and sybil resistance to off-chain indexers; the one empirical study of ERC-8004 found a majority of reviewers on all three chains exhibit coordinated sybil behavior. Proof-of-personhood (Human Passport, World ID) is the only widely deployed sybil-resistance primitive, but it binds to humans, not agents, and its own operators concede it does not eliminate all sybils at the recommended threshold.

### Cited Findings

**ERC-8004 (Trustless Agents) — what the Reputation Registry records and who can write**
- ERC-8004 defines three singleton contracts: Identity, Reputation and Validation registries. The Reputation Registry "stores client feedback as compact on-chain signals and points to richer off-chain files"; each record has "a signed fixed-point score, decimal precision, tags, the rated endpoint, and an off-chain feedback URI"; "NewFeedback events constitute the primary reputation data". Scoring/aggregation is expected both on-chain (composability) and off-chain (sophisticated algorithms). — [EIP-8004 (via search summary)](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004); [QuickNode ERC-8004 Explorer FAQ](https://erc-8004.quicknode.com/faq)
- Who can write: "the submitting wallet is recorded as the client, and anyone with an Ethereum wallet can submit, except the agent owner or an approved operator on reference deployments"; "Sybil resistance is left to indexers." — [QuickNode ERC-8004 Explorer FAQ](https://erc-8004.quicknode.com/faq)
- Feedback pre-authorization (feedbackAuth) exists to limit spam; revocation is supported ("Revoked entries drop out of the feedback score but still count toward the reliability sub-score"); gasless feedback via EIP-7702 is mentioned. [secondary explainers] — [Pinata](https://pinata.cloud/blog/what-is-erc-8004/); [Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/13221214-what-is-erc-8004-the-ethereum-standard-enabling-trustless-ai-agents); [Monad docs](https://docs.monad.xyz/guides/erc-8004)
- Status: one source reports deployment to Ethereum mainnet in January 2026; another says the canonical EIP is still marked Draft. [secondary] — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/erc-8004-explained); [a16z crypto secondary coverage](https://brazencrypto.com/2026/04/a16z-outlines-5-blockchain-solutions-for-ai-agent-infrastructure-gap/)

**ERC-8004 — known shallowness (empirical)**
- "Can Trustless Agents Be Trusted? An Empirical Study of the ERC-8004 Decentralized AI Agent Ecosystem" (arXiv 2606.26028, ~May/June 2026) [preprint]: "The specification leaves Sybil resistance to callers and to off-chain systems that have yet to emerge. On-chain, the only enforced rule is that an owner cannot rate its own agent, a restriction easily bypassed by funding a fresh wallet." It reports that "73.5%, 59.2%, and 90.6% of reviewers across Ethereum, BSC, and Base exhibit coordinated Sybil behavior. After removing flagged feedback, 15.8%, 77.9%, and 86.8% of rated agents, respectively, are left with no valid feedback." It also finds "most ERC-8004 registrations are placeholders." — [arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028)
- Secondary analysis: "Feedback pre-authorization limits spam, but ... a coordinated set of agents can still farm positive feedback among themselves"; "the Registry provides only a minimal foundation: it makes feedback public while leaving scoring, Sybil resistance, and aggregation to off-chain reputation systems"; "Reputation does not yet transfer between chains." A QuillAudits review is cited as recommending "registration bonds with probationary periods, reputation aggregators that score reviewers as well as agents, and zk-proof identity uniqueness for high-stakes use cases." [secondary] — [Medium: ERC-8004 technical/economic/policy analysis](https://medium.com/@gwrx2005/erc-8004-and-the-ethereum-ai-agent-economy-technical-economic-and-policy-analysis-3134290b24d1)
- Concordium (vendor of a competing CIS-8004) argues ERC-8004 leaves an identity gap: no binding of the agent to a verified human/organization. [vendor claim] — [Concordium: ERC-8004 explained](https://www.concordium.com/article/erc-8004-explained-what-it-solves-and-the-gap-it-leaves)

### Inferences
- The draft formula's "VerifiedVolume" and "DisputeRecord" inputs cannot be sourced from ERC-8004 feedback alone: the registry has no notion of verified value transfer or dispute outcome, only client-submitted scores and tags, and its live data is majority-sybil on every chain measured.
- An operator can register thousands of agents under one verified identity (Concordium: 2,000 per account) — any score that is per-agent rather than per-operator will be farmed at the agent layer unless tenure/volume are attributed to the operator.
- No agent-specific leaderboard found (Virtuals aGDP, Recall Rank, Olas) scores disputes, stake or operator KYA; Virtuals' aGDP is a pure volume ranking and thus is exactly the quantity wash trading inflates.

## Key question 2: KYA / agent-vetting products and proposals — what they check and how they bind agent to operator

### Takeaway
The products that exist in October 2026 split into (a) payment-rail attestations (Visa TAP, Mastercard Agent Pay/Verifiable Intent, Skyfire KYAPay, Google AP2 + Trulioo DAP) that bind an agent request to an operator and a consenting user via signatures or JWT delegation chains, (b) enterprise identity/authorization vendors (Keycard, Natoma, SGNL, Strata, Descope, Arcade) that bind an agent to a runtime or a sponsoring user inside one organization, and (c) KYC vendors (Sumsub, Experian) adding "agent-to-human binding." None found publishes a cross-platform, reproducible trust score with an open formula; Experian's "Agent Registry" with "behavior-based trust scores" is the closest, and it is a closed vendor product.

### Inferences
- Binding patterns in the market are: (1) signature-in-request against a central directory (Visa TAP, Web Bot Auth), (2) delegation-chain tokens naming principal/platform/agent (Skyfire, Mastercard Verifiable Intent, Google AP2), (3) KYB/KYC on the developer plus code-integrity hash (Trulioo DAP), (4) runtime/workload attestation (Keycard). A "KYAtier" input for the draft formula could be defined as an ordinal over these: none < self-signed key < directory-listed signer < KYB'd operator with delegation chain < KYB'd operator + attested runtime.
- The payment-rail schemes verify authority to act, not past conduct; only Experian claims a behavioral score, and it is proprietary and not reproducible by third parties. That leaves room for an open score, but also means there is no prior art to copy for reproducibility.

## Key question 3: Academic literature on sybil-resistant reputation (SybilGuard/SybilLimit, cost-of-identity, sybilproofness, stake, decay, two-sided reputation, 2024–2026 LLM-agent papers)

### Takeaway
Two results from the classic literature directly constrain the draft formula: (1) Cheng & Friedman (2005) show any symmetric reputation function is manipulable by sybils and only asymmetric flow/path-based functions can be sybilproof, and (2) Friedman & Resnick (2001) show that with cheap identities the only sustainable equilibrium makes newcomers "pay their dues," with entry fees (stake) as the alternative. The 2026 agent-economics papers add that accumulated reputation increases the one-shot cheating payoff ("Tempting the Agent"), that cross-skill reputation borrowing can be laundered for the cost of one fabricated episode, and that time-decayed Beta models with confidence shrinkage are the current practical baseline (Cairn).

### Cited Findings

**Classic sybil defense**
- SybilGuard/SybilLimit (Yu et al.): bound sybils per "attack edge" between honest and sybil regions; SybilGuard admits O(√n log n) sybils per attack edge, SybilLimit O(log n), "still a log n factor from the theoretical lower bound"; breaking SybilLimit needs "about 2.5 trust relations with each honest user on average." Both assume a fast-mixing social graph and few attack edges; SybilDefender found "the assumption that all social relationships are trusted does not hold on online social networks." [peer-reviewed] — [SybilLimit (Yu et al.)](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~yuhf/yuh-sybillimit.pdf); [Survey: Sybil attacks in social networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.05522); [SybilDefender (TPDS 2013)](https://www.cs.wm.edu/~liqun/paper/tpds13-sybil.pdf)
- Cheng & Friedman, "Sybilproof reputation mechanisms" (SIGCOMM 2005 workshop) [peer-reviewed]: "any symmetric method (such as the one used by Google) can be easily manipulated, while a class of flow and path based methods are more difficult to manipulate." A sybil attack is worthwhile only when "the ratio of the value of the attacker's goal to the cost of identities exceeds a critical value"; "assigning the smallest possible initial reputation for newcomers or an entry fee per identity can help keep identities persistent." — [Stanford seminar abstract](https://or.stanford.edu/seminars/friedman_111605.html); [ResearchGate: Sybilproof reputation mechanisms](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228367243_Sybilproof_reputation_mechanisms)
- Seuken & Parkes (2011) impossibility: "under reasonable assumptions no sybil-proof accounting mechanism exists," with limited robustness via max-flow; Stannat et al. (AAMAS 2021) argue that result does not hold under stricter definitions of sybil benefit. [peer-reviewed] — [Stannat et al., AAMAS 2021](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2021/pdfs/p1263.pdf)
- Flow-based reputation (arXiv 1203.1820): "robustness against attacks and in particular Sybil attacks is obtained by choosing a smaller α." [preprint] — [arXiv 1203.1820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.1820)

**Cost of identity / tenure**
- Friedman & Resnick, "The Social Cost of Cheap Pseudonyms" (JEMS 2001) [peer-reviewed]: cooperation can emerge "through a convention in which newcomers 'pay their dues' by accepting poor treatment from players who have established positive reputations"; "no equilibrium sustains significantly more cooperation than this dues-paying equilibrium"; entry fees "let newcomers be trusted from the start, but ... exclude some low-payoff players"; unchangeable pseudonyms are proposed as an alternative. — [Friedman & Resnick (Gwern mirror)](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/mechanism-design/2001-friedman.pdf); [DeepBlue record](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/bbf2bd84-0474-47bd-b498-b8ac4d912b53/full)

**Stake as collateral / attack-cost ratios**
- AetherWeave (arXiv 2603.23793, 2026): "stake acts both as a surrogate for identity and as a punishment mechanism, since misbehaving entities can have their stake slashed." [preprint] — [arXiv 2603.23793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23793)
- ReCon (Biryukov & Feher): reputation-weighted committee selection where "the cost of Sybil attack increases" and rewards are limited "so that Sybils cannot build up reputation." [peer-reviewed, Pervasive and Mobile Computing] — [ReCon](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/41325/1/biryukov-feher-recon.pdf)
- Cryptoeconomic safety test: protocol is safe "if the cost of carrying out an attack exceeds the profit"; Durvasula & Roughgarden, "Robust Restaking Networks" (arXiv 2407.21785; ITCS 2025) show robustness depends on the buffer between attack cost and profit — "if costs exceed profits by 10%, a loss of 0.1% of stake cannot cascade into more than 1.1% total loss." Symbiotic: required stake rises "as the reward from corruption grows, slashing weakens, or cross-network slashing overlap increases." Noted gap: "slashing burns the attacker's stake but does not compensate harmed users." ### Inferences
- A weighted sum of per-agent tenure, volume, dispute rate, stake and KYA tier is a *symmetric* function of the interaction graph (it does not depend on who the counterparties are relative to a trusted seed). By Cheng & Friedman that class is manipulable by sybils; the volume and dispute terms in particular can be inflated by self-dealing counterparties unless the counterparties' own trust (a flow/EigenTrust-style asymmetric term) weights each transaction.
- "Tempting the Agent" implies that a high score should raise, not lower, the stake requirement: the exit-scam payoff scales with the order limit unlocked, so the bond must scale with band, not be a fixed 10% weight.
- Friedman & Resnick supports a hard newcomer floor (lowest band, small order limit) regardless of tenure points, and supports stake as the way to skip the floor; it does not support linear tenure credit, which is a dues-paying convention only if the dues (poor treatment / low limits) are actually enforced.
- The skill-conditional attack suggests a single global score that is reused across order types/skills can be laundered: evidence in cheap low-value trades should not raise limits for a different, higher-value category.

## Key question 4: Known attacks and empirical results — wash trading, collusion rings, reputation rental/purchase, marketplace defenses, and which design properties have held up

### Takeaway
Volume-based reputation has the worst empirical record: wash trading averaged >70% of reported volume on unregulated crypto exchanges (Cong et al., Management Science 2023) and NFT wash trading is detected by per-token cycle finding (seller→…→same seller) plus short-window repurchase heuristics; account-age ("aged account") markets price established identities at $50–$500, and eBay reputation was shown to be purchasable in 2009. What has held up is (a) outcome-based quality measures that predict bad transactions rather than raw feedback counts (eBay 2026 field experiment), (b) review-mechanism fixes that remove retaliation (Airbnb simultaneous reveal), (c) verified-identity gates layered with cluster analysis, and (d) slashable stake.

### Cited Findings

**Wash trading (crypto/NFT)**
- Cong, Li, Tang & Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading" (Management Science 69(11), 2023; NBER w30783) [peer-reviewed]: statistical tests (first-significant-digit, size rounding, tail distributions) across 29 exchanges; on unregulated exchanges wash trading "averaged over 70% of the reported volume"; fabricated volume "improve[s] exchange ranking and temporarily distort[s] prices"; regulated venues looked like genuine markets. — [NBER w30783](https://nber.org/papers/w30783); [Cornell news](https://business.cornell.edu/news/2021/04/27/cryptocurrency/)
- AnChain.AI, "NFT Wash Trading Detection" (arXiv 2305.01543) [preprint]: builds per-token directed graphs for all transfers and for paid sales; "a cycle is a group of transactions starting from when a seller first sells an NFT token and ending when the same seller buys back the same NFT token"; also flags repurchases within 30 days; across seven collections "on average, 0.14% of transactions, 0.11% of wallets, and 0.16% of tokens" involved. — [arXiv 2305.01543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01543)
- "The Dark Side of NFTs" (arXiv 2312.12544) [preprint]: multiedge directed graph per NFT; notes "the time window for 'short interval' trades has no established empirical value" and proposes time-window segmentation. "Unveiling Wash Trading in Popular NFT Markets" (arXiv 2403.10361) [preprint]: cycle detection in strongly connected components with tunable thresholds. "Beyond the Surface" (Journal of Financial Innovation) [peer-reviewed]: links NFT traces to the full Ethereum transaction graph; warns that directed-graph methods "focus only on transactions that directly involve buying/selling or moving NFT tokens" (i.e., miss off-graph funding links). — [arXiv 2312.12544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12544); [arXiv 2403.10361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10361); [Springer FI](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-025-00766-z)
- von Wachter et al., "NFT Wash Trading: Quantifying suspicious behaviour" (arXiv 2202.03866) [preprint]: per-NFT cycle-finding algorithm that removes edges after each cycle. — [arXiv 2202.03866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.03866)

**Reputation purchase / rental / aged accounts**
- eBay (2009, Int. J. Electronic Business): "unscrupulous vendors ... can easily buy a good reputation and so circumvent recent efforts by the company to prevent feedback fraud." [peer-reviewed] — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/11/091111121742.htm)
- eBay feedback revocation: coercing buyers to revoke negatives "enables low-quality sellers to manipulate their reputations and masquerade as high-quality sellers." [peer-reviewed] — [Strategic Behavior ... Revoking on eBay](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319299479_Strategic_Behavior_in_Online_Reputation_Systems_Evidence_from_Revoking_on_Ebay1)
- Value of reputation: Cabral & Hortaçsu controlled experiment estimated "an eight percent price premium to having 2,000 positive and 1 negative feedback over a reputation of 10 positive and no negative feedbacks" (i.e., the economic prize that farming targets). [peer-reviewed] — [Resnick et al. controlled experiment](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2897980_The_Value_Of_Reputation_On_Ebay_A_Controlled_Experiment)
- Aged-account market: "aged accounts [are] a traded commodity"; "fraud sellers pair aging with account farming, which mass-produces the accounts, and account selling"; premium on aged/verified accounts "is a direct result of hijacked legitimate users"; aged Amazon buyer accounts "$50–$500"; one broker blog states "account age alone means nothing; consistent activity history is what Amazon's trust score actually measures." [industry/vendor; broker blogs are self-interested] — [DomainTools](https://dti.domaintools.com/securitysnacks/securitysnack-account-farmers-and-sellers); [Sardine: account aging](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/account-aging); [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/buy-amazon-aged-accounts/)
- Detection signals for aged accounts: "an account that sits dormant to look established, then suddenly makes a large or risky transaction, especially with a device, IP, or location it has never used"; flag "long stretches of zero activity that then start high-volume actions." [industry] — [Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/account-aging); [DomainTools](https://dti.domaintools.com/securitysnacks/securitysnack-account-farmers-and-sellers)
- Reputation laundering via fresh accounts ("a seller with bad ratings opening a fresh account") and account takeover ("inherits a reputation that a fresh fraudulent account could not establish"). [industry] — [Radar](https://radar.com/blog/5-common-types-of-marketplace-fraud); [Sift](https://sift.com/blog/marketplace-fraud-protection/)

**Marketplace defenses that measurably worked**
- eBay 2026 (Quantitative Marketing and Economics, Springer) [peer-reviewed]: a seller-quality measure "that predicts poor transaction outcomes," used in search ranking in a field experiment, "shifted buyers toward higher-quality sellers and increased subsequent purchasing without reducing contemporaneous conversion." — [Springer QME 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11129-026-09313-1)
- Airbnb simultaneous reveal: see Q3 (reduced retaliation, not adverse selection). — [Fradkin et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bias-and-Reciprocity-in-Online-Reviews:-Evidence-on-Fradkin-Grewal/159485ce42f0462f3f01d6d732cf1e5452588f72)
- Upwork Job Success Score: Upwork help text says it "takes into consideration ... client feedback, contract-ending history, and long-term customer relationships"; commonly reported rolling windows of 6/12/24 months with the best shown (unconfirmed in official docs); private feedback inclusion disputed across guides. [secondary, conflicting] — [Millo](https://millo.co/upwork-job-success-score); [TryVibeWorker](https://tryvibeworker.com/blog/what-upwork-job-success-score-measures)
- Proof-of-personhood layered guidance (Sept 2026): costly signals + PoP gates + wallet cluster analysis. [secondary] — [markaicode](https://markaicode.com/preventing-sybil-attacks-proof-of-personhood-solutions/)
- Agent-specific: the ERC-8004 study's sybil-reviewer rates (73.5%/59.2%/90.6%) are the first empirical measurement of collusion rings in an agent reputation registry. [preprint] — [arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028)

### Inferences
- Design properties that held up: outcome-verified quality (disputes/chargebacks/returns rather than ratings), bilateral-blind feedback, counterparty-weighted (flow-based) credit, verified identity plus cluster analysis, and slashable stake. Properties that did not: raw volume, raw rating counts, account age as a standalone signal, and self-reported capability claims (Olas explicitly removed one).
- For agents, wash trading is cheaper than for NFTs because an operator controls both sides programmatically and can fund fresh wallets; the only cost is the fee/gas per transaction and any stake locked per identity. The NFT cycle-detection literature transfers directly (treat each order as an edge; look for cycles and short-interval back-and-forth between a small wallet cluster), but "Beyond the Surface" warns that funding-graph links outside the order graph must also be analyzed.
- The 30-day repurchase window and "no established empirical value" for short-interval thresholds mean a 12-month scoring window will contain many full wash cycles unless cycles are netted out before volume is counted.

- No 2025–2026 Chainalysis or platform-published wash-trading totals were retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- No Amazon 2025 fake-review enforcement statistics and no Taobao "brushing" study retrieved.
- No academic study on Upwork account renting/JSS manipulation was found; only practitioner blogs.
- Agent Bazaar's numeric sybil detection rates are in the paper's figures but not in any snippet retrieved.

## Key question 5: Regulatory analogies and constraints for a scoring bureau (FCRA, GDPR Art. 22, EU AI Act social scoring, guidance for scoring AI agents/operators)

### Takeaway
No regulator or court has issued guidance on scoring AI agents or their operators as of October 2026. The applicable analogies all key on natural persons: FCRA covers "consumer reports" about a person's "character, general reputation, or personal characteristics" used for eligibility decisions; the CJEU's SCHUFA ruling makes a credit score itself an Article 22 automated decision when it is "decisively" relied on; and the EU AI Act Article 5(1)(c) bans social scoring of people by public and private actors. A bureau that scores *agents* whose operators are companies is largely outside these regimes, but any sole-trader operator or human-bound KYA tier pulls the score into GDPR/FCRA territory.

### Cited Findings
- FCRA scope: a "consumer report" is "any communication that is furnished by a 'consumer reporting agency'" bearing on "character, general reputation, or personal characteristics" when used for eligibility decisions; "the definition is broader than many businesses assume." [law-firm commentary] — [Wiley: FCRA who is covered](https://www.wileyconnect.com/ftc-consumer-protection-and-privacy-enforcement-series-the-fair-credit-reporting-act-who-is-covered-and-how-to-comply)
- AI does not exempt: "if your AI model uses consumer data to generate a score, risk assessment, or prediction that informs a covered decision, the FCRA likely applies." [advocacy-style commentary] — [ratedwithai](https://ratedwithai.com/blog/fcra-ai-credit-scoring-compliance-2026)
- Litigation: a January 2026 class action against Eightfold AI alleges its applicant scores may be "consumer reports" under FCRA (unresolved). The CFPB's October 2024 circular on algorithmic worker scores has "since [been] withdrawn." [law-firm/secondary] — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/employers-vendors-fcra-obligations-when-using-workplace-ai-tools-compliance-guide); [CorpIntel](https://corpintel.com/resources/blog/ai-hiring-tools-fcra-consumer-reports/)
- Financial institutions "will want to ensure that those scores are not 'credit scores' under the FCRA, which would trigger adverse-action disclosures." [law-firm] — [Venable, Feb 2026](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/02/ai-in-financial-services-popular-use-cases)
- GDPR Art. 22 / SCHUFA (CJEU C-634/21): the ruling "extends the scope of automated individual decision-making to credit scoring and shifts responsibility toward credit reference agencies as the decision-maker"; test is whether "the subsequent final decision ... was decisively based on the preceding automated decision"; but it "did not hold that every AI system producing a probability value, risk score or class assignment about a person sits inside Article 22(1)." [law-firm + Springer ERA Forum article] — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-rules-that-a-credit-score-constitutes-automated-decision-making-under-the-gdpr); [Springer: AI credit scoring at the intersection of GDPR and AI Act](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12027-025-00865-5); [FPF ADM report](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/FPF-ADM-Report-R2-singles.pdf)
- EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(c) social scoring ban "reaches both public and private actors"; targets scoring people "by social behavior or personal traits, where the result is unfair treatment," especially "when the information is drawn from multiple unrelated social contexts or when the resulting treatment is disproportionate"; conduct below that threshold still faces GDPR profiling/ADM rules. [FPF analysis; Commission guidance summary] — [FPF: social scoring red lines](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-the-eu-ai-act-unpacking-social-scoring-as-a-prohibited-ai-practice); [CMS: prohibited practices](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/eu-ai-act/prohibited-ai-practices-and-high-risk-ai-systems)
- Contestability: a 2026 preprint argues "Explainable AI Isn't Enough" and calls for algorithmic contestability mechanisms beyond explanations. [preprint] — [arXiv 2605.16041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16041)
- KYA products that bind agents to a *verified consumer* (Sumsub, Experian Agent Trust) explicitly create a person-linked record. [vendor] — [Experian Agent Trust](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260430719198/en/Experian-Announces-Agent-Trust-to-Power-Trusted-AI-Driven-Commerce); [Sumsub](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sumsubs-ai-agent-verification-introduces-agent-to-human-binding-to-establish-human-accountability-in-ai-302673467.html)

### Inferences
- If the bureau's KYAtier input stores or implies a verified human (consumer) behind the agent, and the score gates that person's ability to transact, the SCHUFA logic (score = the decision when decisively relied on) and FCRA's "general reputation" prong both plausibly attach; the safe design is to score the *operator entity* (KYB'd legal person) and the agent key, and to keep human-binding as a boolean attestation rather than an input to the number.
- The AI Act's social-scoring concern about aggregating "multiple unrelated social contexts" argues against importing social-graph reputation (Farcaster/Neynar, Passport stamps) into a commercial agent score.
- FCRA-style obligations the architect should plan for regardless of legal trigger (because counterparties will demand them): dispute/correction process, adverse-action notice (why a band was denied), permissible-purpose access control, and retention limits consistent with the 12-month window.

### Gaps
- No regulator guidance, enforcement action or court decision specifically about scoring AI agents or their operators was found.
- No source addressed whether an AI Act "operator"/"deployer" of an agent is the scored subject.
- US state-level laws (e.g., Colorado AI Act, CCPA ADMT rules) were not searched.

## Key question 6: Evaluation of the draft formula against the literature

Draft: score = 1000 × (0.25·Tenure + 0.30·VerifiedVolume + 0.25·DisputeRecord + 0.10·Stake + 0.10·KYAtier) − penalties; five bands; 12-month windows; target: farming to "Trusted" costs ≥ 10× the order limit it unlocks.

### Takeaway
The formula's structure (symmetric weighted sum, volume as the largest weight, tenure second, stake at 10%) is the configuration the literature says is weakest: symmetric functions are sybil-manipulable (Cheng & Friedman), volume is the most-faked signal in every market measured (Cong et al.; NFT studies; ERC-8004 study), tenure is a traded commodity (aged-account markets), and a fixed small stake weight cannot deliver the 10× attack-cost target when the unlocked order limit grows with band ("Tempting the Agent"; restaking cost-of-corruption models). The 10× cost target is well-grounded as a design principle but is only achievable if cost is dominated by slashable stake and verified-counterparty volume rather than by points that can be bought.

### Cited Findings (mapping evidence to each term)
- Symmetric weighted sums are manipulable; asymmetric flow/path-based scores are harder to game. — [Cheng & Friedman 2005 (seminar abstract)](https://or.stanford.edu/seminars/friedman_111605.html)
- VerifiedVolume (0.30): unless "verified" means counterparty-trust-weighted and cycle-netted, volume was >70% fake on unregulated venues and is wash-traded via seller→…→seller cycles and <30-day repurchases. — [Cong et al.](https://nber.org/papers/w30783); [AnChain.AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01543)
- Reviewer-side collusion dominates the only live agent registry (73.5%/59.2%/90.6% sybil reviewers). — [arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028)
- Tenure (0.25): aged identities are sold ($50–$500 for Amazon buyer accounts); Amazon's practice reportedly weights "consistent activity history," not age. — [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/buy-amazon-aged-accounts/); [Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/account-aging)
- Newcomer handling: the sustainable convention under cheap identities is newcomers "pay their dues"; entry fees substitute. — [Friedman & Resnick 2001](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/mechanism-design/2001-friedman.pdf)
- Stake (0.10): temptation to defect scales with accumulated reputation; "targeted staking" removes it. — [Tempting the Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02992)
- Attack-cost framing: safe iff cost of attack > profit; robustness depends on the buffer between them (a 10% buffer bounds cascades). The draft's 10× target is a much larger buffer than the 1.1× analyzed in restaking, which is conservative. — [Durvasula & Roughgarden](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21785v1); [Symbiotic](https://blog.symbiotic.fi/modeling-target-stake-requirements)
- DisputeRecord (0.25): complaint-based penalties change LLM-seller behavior in simulation; outcome-based quality (predicting bad transactions) outperformed raw feedback at eBay in a field experiment. — [arXiv 2607.28330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28330); [Springer QME 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11129-026-09313-1)
- Dispute records are gameable from the reviewer side (eBay revocation coercion) unless feedback is bilateral-blind. — [Revoking on eBay](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319299479_Strategic_Behavior_in_Online_Reputation_Systems_Evidence_from_Revoking_on_Ebay1); [Fradkin et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bias-and-Reciprocity-in-Online-Reviews:-Evidence-on-Fradkin-Grewal/159485ce42f0462f3f01d6d732cf1e5452588f72)
- Windows/decay: practical systems use geometric forgetting (Storj λ=0.987) or continuous half-life decay with confidence shrinkage (Cairn, 3-day half-life); shrinkage toward the prior for low-evidence entities is standard. — [Storj forum](https://forum.storj.io/t/tuning-audit-scoring/14084/16); [arXiv 2609.19502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19502)
- Single global score across skills/order types can be laundered: cheap-skill evidence "can take over routing for the cost of one fabricated episode." — [arXiv 2606.14200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14200)
- KYAtier (0.10): one verified account can hold 2,000 agents on Concordium; KYA binding schemes verify authority, not conduct. — [Concordium Agent Registry](https://www.concordium.com/article/the-agent-registry-explained); [Experian](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260430719198/en/Experian-Announces-Agent-Trust-to-Power-Trusted-AI-Driven-Commerce)

### Inferences (explicit contradictions and recommended changes)
- CONTRADICTION 1 — Volume weight: giving VerifiedVolume the largest weight (0.30) contradicts Cong et al., the NFT literature and the ERC-8004 study, where volume/feedback counts are the primary farmed quantity. Fix: count only volume with counterparties whose own score is above a floor, net out cycles (A→B→A within N days) and cluster-linked counterparties, and cap the volume contribution from any single counterparty cluster (e.g., no cluster > 10–20% of credited volume). Make the volume term asymmetric (EigenTrust-style flow from pre-trusted seeds) rather than a symmetric sum.
- CONTRADICTION 2 — Tenure weight: 0.25 on tenure rewards exactly what aged-account markets sell and what "sleeper" agents do for free. Fix: tenure should be *active* tenure (months with verified, dispute-free volume above a minimum) and should saturate quickly; dormant-then-burst patterns should trigger a hold, per the Sardine/DomainTools signals.
- CONTRADICTION 3 — Stake as a fixed 10% weight: "Tempting the Agent" and the cost-of-corruption literature say the bond must scale with the payoff of defection, i.e., with the order limit of the band. A 10% weight cannot make farming cost ≥ 10× the unlocked limit for high bands; only a band-conditional minimum stake (stake ≥ k × order limit, slashable on adjudicated dispute) does. Treat stake as a gate per band, not a weighted point source.
- CONTRADICTION 4 — Symmetric linear combination: by Cheng & Friedman, a sybil can always improve a symmetric score by creating identities that interact with it. Tenure, volume and dispute terms are all symmetric as drafted. At least one term (volume) must be computed asymmetrically from a trusted seed set for the score to have any sybil-proofness argument.
- CONTRADICTION 5 — Per-agent scoring: KYA products bind agents to operators; the 2,000-agents-per-account precedent means a per-agent score lets one operator spread disputes across many identities and concentrate volume on one. Attribute DisputeRecord and penalties to the operator entity (and propagate to all its agents), while tenure/volume accrue per agent.
- CONTRADICTION 6 — Single global score: per the skill-conditional attack, a band earned on low-value order types should not unlock high-value types; either scope bands per order class or require direct evidence in the target class.
- The 10× cost target is defensible and stricter than cryptoeconomic norms (which analyze ~1.1× buffers), but "cost" must be measured as slashable stake + non-recoverable fees + verified-counterparty volume that itself required real value transfer; reputation points obtained from colluding counterparties cost only gas and should count as zero in the attack-cost accounting.
- DisputeRecord is the best-supported term (eBay field experiment; complaint-penalty simulations) provided disputes are adjudicated outcomes, not ratings, and are bilateral-blind.
- Reproducibility/portability: no existing product publishes a reproducible open formula; ERC-8004 gives a portable *data substrate* but not sybil-filtered inputs. A reproducible score needs a published seed set, cluster-detection rules and decay constants, which is compatible with the architect's goal but has no precedent to cite as "proven in production."
- Regulatory: scoring the operator (legal entity) rather than a bound human keeps the bureau out of FCRA/SCHUFA scope in most cases; adding dispute/correction and adverse-action-style explanations is cheap insurance.
