Where a claim rests only on a search-result snippet of a secondary blog, it is flagged "(secondary)". Everything below is dated and labelled announced / pilot / live where sources allow. ## Key Question 1: Direct competitors (KYA providers, agent credit scores, reputation oracles, trust registries, risk feeds)

### Takeaway
No entity yet occupies the "neutral credit bureau for agents" position on Base: the crowded field splits into (a) KYA/identity vendors tied to a payment rail (Skyfire, Sumsub, Trulioo, Concordium, Catena), (b) a long tail of mostly single-developer ERC-8004 reputation oracles on Base (RNWY, 8k4, Helixa, DJD, MainStreet, SENTINEL) with no disclosed funding and self-reported traction, and (c) escrow/dispute efforts (ERC-8183, Virtuals ACP, Internet Court, Synmerco) that explicitly do not score counterparties. Nobody found combines KYA + trust score + escrow evaluation + disputes, and academic work finds existing ERC-8004 reputation "can be manipulated at minimal cost."

### Cited Findings

**ERC-8004 (the substrate most Base competitors build on)**
- ERC-8004 is Status: Draft, created 2025-08-13, authored by Marco De Rossi (MetaMask), Davide Crapis (EF), Jordan Ellis, Erik Reppel (Coinbase); Identity Registry is an ERC-721, Reputation Registry stores signed int128 feedback with tag1/tag2; "more complex reputation aggregation will happen off-chain"; "Payments are orthogonal to this protocol and not covered here"; validator incentives/slashing are out of scope; the spec itself warns sybil attacks "could inflate fake agents' reputation" and expects reputation systems to filter/weight by reviewer — [ERC-8004 spec (ethereum/ERCs)](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-8004.md)
- Deployed as per-chain singletons (Identity `0x8004A169…`, Reputation `0x8004BAa1…`) on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, Linea, Scroll, Avalanche, BNB, Celo, Gnosis, Monad, Abstract, Mantle, Soneium, Taiko; audited by Cyfrin, Nethermind, EF Security; Identity and Reputation registries are final, Validation Registry "still under revision" — [awesome-erc8004](https://github.com/sudeepb02/awesome-erc8004)
- Official best-practices doc: ERC-8004 "provides only the feedback data layer"; each project defines its own filtering/ranking; sybil resistance is not discussed; expects "watchtowers or infrastructure providers" to probe agents on a cadence and publish reliability signals; roles contemplated include "insurers, and auditor networks" — [erc-8004/best-practices Reputation.md](https://github.com/erc-8004/best-practices/blob/main/Reputation.md)
- Empirical study "Can Trustless Agents Be Trusted?" (arXiv 2606.26028, 2026) concludes registries "succeed at providing public identities and feedback, but these do not necessarily establish trust"; registration counts and feedback volume are "poor indicators of maturity when many identities are placeholders and much of the reputation is coordinated" — [arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26028) (snippet only; full text not fetchable)
- A separate paper reports feedback scores "can be manipulated at minimal cost" — cited via [Brazen Crypto summary of a16z/ERC-8004 research](https://brazencrypto.com/2026/04/a16z-outlines-5-blockchain-solutions-for-ai-agent-infrastructure-gap/) (secondary)
- Indexer counts vary widely: Agent Arena 22,000+ agents across 16 EVM chains + Solana; 8k4 indexes 106,996 (Base 33,939; BSC 44,020; Ethereum 29,037); RNWY 150,000+ across 12 EVM chains + Solana plus 4.5M+ commerce jobs from Olas and Virtuals — [awesome-erc8004](https://github.com/sudeepb02/awesome-erc8004); testnet 8004scan showed 316,000+ registered agents — [8004scan testnet](https://testnet.8004scan.io/)

### Inferences
- The "bureau" slot on Base is open but contested from below: at least a dozen reputation oracles already publish scores on Base, nearly all unfunded, single-team, self-reported, and each with its own incompatible scale (0–100, 0–1000, 50–95, AAA–D, 300–850). Their existence is evidence of demand for an aggregator, and also evidence that score publication alone is not a moat.
- The structural gap ERC-8004 leaves (off-chain aggregation, reviewer weighting, sybil resistance, watchtower probing, insurer/auditor roles) is exactly the bureau function; the spec authors and best-practices doc explicitly anticipate third parties filling it.
- No found product combines KYA of the operator + reputation score + escrow evaluation + dispute resolution. KYA vendors stop at identity; oracles stop at scores; ERC-8183/ACP stop at "reject is final"; Internet Court does disputes without identity/scoring. The honest "why us" is integration of the four functions and neutrality across rails (Skyfire/Trulioo/Visa/Mastercard are each tied to a rail or a compliance product).
- The credibility risk is the same one the arXiv study raises: scores built from ERC-8004 feedback are manipulable; a bureau's value rests on escrow-bound (settled-transaction) reviews and operator KYA, which is the pattern Ballast and Amana prototype but have not shipped.

## Overall "who occupies the position" summary and honest "why us" (synthesis across questions)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the "credit bureau for agents on Base" seat is empty but ringed: identity is being claimed by rail-affiliated KYA vendors (Skyfire, Trulioo, Sumsub, Concordium, Catena) and platforms (Visa TAP, Mastercard, Entra, Okta, Cloudflare); scoring is attempted by a dozen unfunded ERC-8004 oracles on Base with incompatible scales; escrow exists (ERC-8183/Virtuals ACP, Synmerco) but disclaims disputes; disputes are being organized by Internet Court without identity or scoring. The defensible claim for a neutral bureau is (1) rail- and platform-neutrality, (2) scores bound to settled escrow outcomes and operator KYA rather than free-form ERC-8004 feedback (which academic work shows is cheaply manipulable), and (3) packaging the four functions fraud vendors and PSPs currently assemble from separate suppliers.

### Cited Findings
- See Q1–Q5 above; key anchors: ERC-8004's explicit delegation of aggregation to off-chain parties — [ERC-8004](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-8004.md); ERC-8183's "no dispute resolution" — [ERC-8183](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md); Coinbase's missing identity primitive — [Autheo](https://www.autheo.com/signals/coinbase-ai-agents-future-crypto-april-2026); Mastercard score as detection-only and undisclosed — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/mastercards-new-agent-detection-score-asks-a-question-no-card-network-has-asked-before/); Arkose 2.3% verified-ID — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/97-7-of-legitimate-ai-agent-sessions-carry-no-verified-id-arkose-finds/); non-convergence of KYA token/score formats — [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/know-your-agent-kya-tcpa-replacement-compliance/).

### Inferences
- Nearest conceptual competitors to AgentBureau's full scope: RNWY (multi-registry score + Base oracle + sybil detection, but no KYA/escrow/disputes), Ballast agent-trust and Amana (escrow-bound reputation, prototypes only), Synmerco (escrow + disputes + insurance on Base, no scoring), Concordium (verified-owner identity, own L1), Skyfire (KYA + rail, no bureau).
- Highest-probability incumbents to enter the position: Coinbase/x402 Foundation (owns Base rail, lacks identity/reputation), Mastercard/Visa (already scoring agents inside their networks), and a16z-backed Catena (bank + DID kit).

### Gaps
- No direct evidence of any entity marketing itself as a "credit bureau for agents" or "agent risk feed" with disclosed customers, revenue, or funding; the closest terms found ("Agent Credit Score," "C-Score," "Trust Oracle") belong to unfunded or hackathon-stage projects.
