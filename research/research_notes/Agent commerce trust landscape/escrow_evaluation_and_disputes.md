## Key Question 1: ERC-8183 ("Agentic Commerce") — status, authors, state machine, evaluator role, reference implementations, deployments, audits, known issues

### Takeaway
ERC-8183 is a Draft ERC (created 2026-02-25, merged into ethereum/ERCs 2026-03-05) by Davide Crapis (Ethereum Foundation) and three Virtuals Protocol engineers; it defines a minimal six-state job escrow in which a single per-job `evaluator` address alone can `complete` or `reject` a submitted job, with explicitly "no dispute resolution or arbitration". There is no audited canonical deployment: the `erc-8183/base-contracts` reference lists no deployments or audits and has open issues claiming 7 HIGH findings and a fund-locking storage bug in a BSC fork; the "deployed-and-used" instances are third-party forks (ClawWork on Base, BNB Chain APEX on BSC, PactEscrow on Arbitrum, Arc testnet) whose usage figures are self-reported.

State machine (normative text)
- Six states: Open (created; budget not yet set/funded), Funded (budget escrowed; provider may submit), Submitted (work delivered; only evaluator may complete or reject), Completed (terminal; escrow paid to provider minus optional fee), Rejected (terminal; refund to client), Expired (terminal; refund to client). Transitions: Open→Funded (client `fund` after `setBudget`); Open→Rejected (client `reject`); Funded→Submitted (provider `submit`); Funded→Rejected (evaluator `reject`); Funded→Expired and Submitted→Expired (anyone `claimRefund` once `expiredAt` passed); Submitted→Completed (evaluator `complete`); Submitted→Rejected (evaluator `reject`). "No other transitions are valid." — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)
- Functions: `createJob(provider, evaluator, expiredAt, description, hook?)` (reverts if evaluator is zero or expiry not in future; provider may be zero); `setProvider` (client only, while Open, provider unset); `setBudget` (client or provider, while Open); `fund(jobId, expectedBudget, optParams?)` (client only; reverts if provider unset, budget zero, or budget != expectedBudget — front-running protection); `submit(jobId, deliverable bytes32, …)` (provider, while Funded; deliverable is a hash/CID/commitment); `complete(jobId, reason, …)` (evaluator, while Submitted; fee only on completion); `reject(jobId, reason, …)`; `claimRefund(jobId)` (anyone, Funded or Submitted, `block.timestamp >= expiredAt`; not hookable) — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)
- Evaluator role: "One address per job, set at creation. In Submitted, only the evaluator may complete or reject. In Funded, it may reject. It may be the client, or a smart contract running arbitrary checks such as verifying a ZK proof." — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)
- Hooks: `IACPHook` has exactly two functions, `beforeAction(jobId, selector, data)` (may revert to block) and `afterAction(jobId, selector, data)` (revert rolls back whole tx); hookable functions are `setProvider`, `setBudget`, `fund`, `submit`, `complete`, `reject`; `claimRefund` is deliberately not hookable; hooks are client-chosen and trusted, should be gas-limited and not upgradeable after job creation — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)
- Events (SHOULD emit): JobCreated, ProviderSet, BudgetSet, JobFunded, JobSubmitted, JobCompleted(jobId, evaluator, reason), JobRejected(jobId, rejector, reason), JobExpired, PaymentReleased, Refunded — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)
- Security Considerations state verbatim that the evaluator "is trusted once a job is Submitted and can complete or reject arbitrarily", suggests "reputation or staking for high-value jobs", and: "No dispute resolution or arbitration; reject/expire is final." Optional extensions: ERC-8004 reputation interop, ERC-2771 meta-transactions, ERC-2612 permit — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)

Spec vs. the in-spec reference code (discrepancies documented in the spec file itself)
- The spec's embedded reference `AgenticCommerce.sol` is UUPS-upgradeable with AccessControl and transient reentrancy guards, adds a platform fee and an evaluator fee in basis points (combined cap 10000), a hook whitelist, a 5-minute minimum expiry, and events `EvaluatorFeePaid` and `HookWhitelistUpdated` not listed in the spec. The reference `fund(jobId, optParams)` lacks `expectedBudget` (so the front-running check is absent); reference restricts `setBudget` to the provider; reference `submit` permits calls from Open with a budget set — [ERC-8183 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethereum/ERCs/master/ERCS/erc-8183.md)

## Key Question 2: Other agent escrow / job-market contracts (Virtuals ACP, Olas, APEX, PactEscrow, AgentWorks, Nevermined/Skyfire/x402, Fetch.ai, Recall, Payman, Coinbase AgentKit) — evaluator selection and disagreement handling

### Takeaway
Among live systems, only Virtuals ACP (and the ERC-8183 forks derived from it) uses an explicit third-party evaluator, and the buyer picks that evaluator at job creation with no appeal; Olas' Mech Marketplace is prepaid request/deliver with no evaluator or dispute path; BNB's APEX replaces the evaluator with an optimistic "silence = approve, dispute = whitelisted-voter quorum" policy; PactEscrow uses creator approval or a pre-agreed arbitrator with timeout auto-release. No deployed agent marketplace found has a multi-tier dispute process with bonds and appeals.

### Inferences
- Every live system found resolves "disagreement" by one of three primitives: (a) a single pre-named evaluator's verdict is final (ACP/ERC-8183), (b) optimistic auto-approve with a small whitelisted voter set as the only veto (APEX), or (c) timeout auto-release to the seller (PactEscrow, Kleros Escrow deadline execution). The proposed bureau's three tiers (rules → bonded human review → bonded appeal) are not present in any of them, which makes it differentiated but also means there is no field data on how agents behave under bonded appeal.
- Because ACP lets the buyer name itself as evaluator by default, a neutral evaluator service has to be "sold" to buyers as a reason sellers will accept the job; the AgentWorks adapter shows the integration surface is just "hold the evaluator key and call complete/reject".

## Key Question 3: Decentralized dispute resolution (Kleros, UMA, Aragon Court/Celeste, Reality.eth, optimistic-settlement patterns) — reuse as human-review or appeal layer, cost, time

### Takeaway
Kleros v2 on Arbitrum One is the only live, general-purpose human-juror court with an agent-facing on-ramp: it created an "Agentic Commerce Court" (court 34) in August 2026 with 0.00027 ETH/juror fees, sub-hour evidence/commit/vote periods and a 36-hour appeal period, a court jump to the human Commerce Court (0.018 ETH/juror) at 7 jurors, and a hand-issued MCP/JSON-RPC "Dispute Service" so an agent can file without deploying an arbitrable; appeals follow a 2n+1 juror rule with the challenger paying 2× the appeal cost. ### Inferences

## Key Question 4: Delivery evidence and oracles (carrier tracking, proof-of-delivery, zkTLS, Chainlink Functions) and precedents for rule-based auto-ruling (eBay MBG, Visa/Stripe chargebacks)

### Takeaway
zkTLS (Reclaim proxy-witness, TLSNotary MPC, Opacity MPC+TEE) is the only general way for a buyer to prove a carrier/retailer page without API access, with a 2026 formal model (zkPoPE) and sub-10-second mobile proofs, but no shipment-tracking provider templates or dispute integrations were found; carrier-API-to-chain patterns (EasyPost/AfterShip via Chainlink Functions) exist only as hackathon/academic prototypes. ### Inferences
- For physical goods ≤ $200 the realistic evidence stack is: carrier tracking status fetched server-side by the bureau (not an on-chain oracle), optionally hardened by a zkTLS proof generated by whichever party is disputing (buyer proving "not delivered"/retailer page, seller proving carrier "delivered" page). ## Key Question 5: Legal and regulatory — money transmission (FinCEN), state escrow licensing, consumer-protection implications of binding automated rulings, Taiwan

### Takeaway
Under FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance the hinge is "total independent control" over the value; a purely non-custodial contract where the operator cannot move funds is argued (Cravath/Tornado Cash analysis, Samourai filing) to fall outside money transmission, but a bureau that holds the evaluator key that releases escrow is closer to the line, and online escrow is separately licensed at state level (California DFPI "internet escrow agent", min $25,000 bond; several states treat online escrow as money transmission). Binding automated rulings raise GDPR Art. 22 / EU AI Act Annex III(8)(a) issues in the EU. Taiwan passed a Virtual Asset Service Act on 2026-06-30 (seven permission-based license categories incl. custody and transfer; up to 7 years' imprisonment for unlicensed operation; effective Q1 2027 at earliest) and separately regulates e-payment/escrow-like collection under the Electronic Payment Institutions Act (NT$100M–500M capital).

### Cited Findings

US federal — FinCEN
- FinCEN's May 9, 2019 guidance (FIN-2019-G001) applies BSA money-transmission rules to CVC business models and "adds no new requirements", consolidating rulings since 2011 — [FinCEN CVC guidance PDF](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf)
- Four-factor wallet test: who owns the value, where it is stored, whether the owner deals directly with the payment system, and whether the intermediary has "total independent control"; hosted wallet providers are MSBs, unhosted generally not (via search summary of Katten alert) — [Katten advisory](https://katten.com/files/646592_2019_06_26_frm_fs_fincen_publishes_guidance_pertaining_to__certain_business_models_involving_convertible_virtual_currencies.pdf)

## Key Question 6: Economics — bond sizes, appeal fees, false-dispute deterrence, and whether bonds suppress legitimate disputes

### Takeaway
Observed deterrence parameters cluster around symmetric or asymmetric loser-pays bonds (Kleros challenger pays 2× the appeal cost; UMA disputer bond equals proposer bond with half paid to the winning disputer; Aragon Court required collateral to appeal but not to open a dispute; Upwork charges a flat ~$291–337 arbitration fee that can exceed the claim), and Visa's 2026 VAMP adds an $8/dispute merchant fee at a 1.5% ratio. No empirical study was found measuring whether bonds suppress legitimate disputes in DAO courts; the closest evidence is a 2023 experimental study on arbitration clauses and claim suppression and Kleros' own admission that error rates are unstudied.

### Inferences
- Existing DAO-court bonds are proportional to juror cost, not to claim size: Kleros' Agentic Commerce Court makes a 3-juror case cost ≈ 0.0008 ETH and an appeal ≈ 0.004 ETH regardless of the amount at stake, which is far below the proposed 2% bond on anything over ≈ $20–200 in ETH terms; conversely Upwork's flat fee can exceed small claims entirely. A 2% bond with a floor (so a $200 dispute posts more than $4) and a cap would sit between those extremes.
- "Only the paying side wins" (Upwork, Kleros fee timeout) is the most widely used false-dispute deterrent in practice and needs no quantitative bond sizing; the bureau's single bonded appeal mirrors Aragon's "appeal + confirm appeal" two-step.
- There is no field evidence either way on suppression of legitimate agent disputes; the bureau could generate it by logging dispute rates per bond level.
